# ED-15 — Commitment and rescue plays: calendar commitment, deposit close, trial terms, payment splits, ghost text, same-call upsell

`source: stated` (S-14, section "Discovery Call Workflow + Sales Meeting & Closing Deals",
lessons 81, 83, 84, 88, 90, 96; and "Overcoming Objections Mastery", lessons 101, 105, 106)
unless marked. These are the moves he uses when the ED-07 call does not end in a clean yes.
Several are flagged in open edges; read those before teaching any of them.

## Purpose
Keep a deal moving when the prospect is undecided, cash-constrained, wary of a long term, or
has gone quiet — by getting a small, specific commitment instead of an open "I'll get back
to you".

## When to use
After objection handling (ED-14) has not produced a yes; when a follow-up is needed; when a
prospect stops replying; after free or trial work has been delivered.

## Inputs
The ED-07 call notes (pains, goal, gap); a calendar; a client-journey board you can
customise in a couple of hours; your three-term package sheet; payment link; agreement.

## Steps (by play, in the order they occur in a deal)

### P1 · Calendar commitment (lessons 81, 101, 105, 106)
1. Never accept "I'll call you back" or "call me later".
2. Offer a specific time instead, framed as saving both sides the chase: you are not
   reliably free at random times.
3. If they miss it, follow up asking what happened and whether to rebook — from a scheduled
   meeting, not from chasing.
Why (his): it keeps you out of a "needy" frame and replaces a call-back that "almost no one
ever" makes.

### P2 · Deposit close (lesson 83, with a live clip)
Used only after the full call and objection handling, for the prospect stuck between buying
and not buying, or when a partner is present but will "run" if the call ends.
1. Name their uncertainty (clarity, worry, not seeing how it helps).
2. Offer to build a custom plan for them — his is a project board mapping their client
   journey, content ideas and each step — and ask whether that would help. Most say yes.
3. Because it costs you a few hours, ask for a **fully refundable deposit**; if asked how
   much, "whatever you like — most clients do $500–1,000".
4. Book the review call within a day or two. **Show the plan only on that call** — never send
   it — so the deposit secures attendance.
5. On the call, they either move forward, or ask for the deposit back, which you refund.
His theory: about 10% buy anyway, 10% never buy, and the middle 80% need an intermediate
step; the deposit is that step. It also flushes out a prospect not willing to put down a
refundable amount — "something else is going on".

### P3 · Authorize the agreement (lesson 84)
He replaces "sign the contract" with "authorize the agreement so that we can [their
outcome]", because "contract" sounds like being locked in. The agreement is sent and signed
inside the CRM (lessons 92, 93: a proposal document with both parties' signature fields).

### P4 · Payment split (lessons 88, 90)
1. Default is payment upfront ("most of our clients pay upfront").
2. If the prospect wants it but cash flow is the issue, offer to split, framed as custom:
   "if you're open to an honest conversation about finances, we can see what we can do".
3. Ask what they could put down without hurting payroll or cash flow; settle a monthly
   amount (in his role-play, a six-month package split into equal months).
4. Bill every 30 days from the start date — no waiting for the first of the month.
5. Front-load what the prospect said mattered most (in his example, headshots first).

### P5 · "Love it or leave it" trial term (lesson 88)
When the objection is being locked in for 6–12 months:
1. Clarify the concern behind it ("are you worried it may not work?").
2. Offer a short committed term — two months, after which they decide — or the three-month
   package as the trial; meet them where they are rather than cornering them.
3. Say you don't want their money if it isn't working.
He says every client he has put on such a trial has re-signed `[tag: assumption] stated by
S-14 · lesson 88`. The pricing rewards longer terms, so the short trial costs them more per
month.

### P6 · Same-call upsell after a free case study (lesson 88)
After recording a free case-study project with a client, stop recording and ask where they
want to go from here; offer a small discount on monthly packages. Then the normal close:
"does doing this consistently help you grow?", show packages, "which are you leaning
towards?", ROI break-even (their average client value against the price), "why not keep
doing what you're doing?", "what are you leaving on the table?", then P4/P5 for objections.
His reasoning: people who already received work trust you and "feel like they owe you".

### P7 · Ghost text (lesson 96)
A last attempt when a prospect stops replying after a few days:
1. From a phone (he uses a personal iPhone, not the CRM number), send a short "is this
   [name]? want to make sure I have the right number".
2. When they ask who it is, name yourself and the topic and ask whether it's a bad time.
3. Now that they've replied, you usually get a decision either way. Wait a few days first;
   do not "blow them up".

## Decision points
- Stuck after objection handling → P2. Cash flow is the only issue → P4. Term length is the
  issue → P5. Gone quiet → P7, once. Any stall → P1 first.
- Will not put down a refundable deposit → stop following up; find the real issue or move on.

## Outputs
A booked follow-up, a deposit and a review call, a signed agreement and first payment, or a
clear no.

## Open edges
- **Ghost text is a pretext.** Messaging from an unrecognised number with "just checking I
  have the right number" disguises a sales follow-up; sending marketing texts from a personal
  phone outside the CRM also bypasses the opt-out and registration rules in ED-09. Do not
  teach it as given. Our version: one honest message from the registered number that names
  you and offers a clear "no thanks" option.
- **Deposit close**: he says it "plays on reciprocity and makes them feel bad" (lesson 83).
  Keep the useful part (a real plan the prospect asked for, a review call) and drop the guilt
  lever. "Fully refundable" must be written down, with how and when it is refunded, before
  money is taken — otherwise it is a misleading term.
- **Same-call upsell after free work**: "play on that so hard" and "they feel like they owe
  you" (lesson 88) is pressure built on a gift. Overlaps H-15. Ours: the free work has a
  stated scope, and the upsell offer stands on the work's results.
- **"Authorize the agreement"**: softer wording is fine only if the prospect is told plainly
  that it is a binding agreement and for how long. Wording chosen to hide commitment is a
  consumer-protection risk.
- **Trial term**: "I know we're going to knock it out of the park" is an assurance of
  results; drop it. The trial's exit terms must be in the agreement.
- **Payment splits** taken by card on a call: pause the recording while card details are
  read (his closer does this, lesson 90) and prefer a payment link.
- His figures come from a video-production agency; whether they carry to affiliate or other
  agency offers is unmeasured.

## Canon map
`source: derived`. All sit at the end of the seven-step script: P2, P4, P5 are variations of
**Offer Stack** (a smaller first step, a different payment shape, a shorter term) and **Close**;
P1 and P7 are follow-up mechanics outside the call. In I.A.C.E. terms, P2 is a structured
**escalate** — but toward a smaller commitment rather than an honest stop. P6 is ACE
**Expansion** (an existing relationship turned into a retainer), entered through free work
rather than through the seven-step script. Our test, not the canon's: a commitment device is
acceptable only if the prospect would still agree to it after seeing how it works — P7 and the
guilt part of P2 fail it.
