# ED-06 — Organic direct-message outreach

`source: stated` (S-13 · "Messaging - How To Book Calls From Chat/DM/Text Organically";
S-14, the "Prospecting" videos, lessons 43 and 51–57) unless marked. Where the lists come from
is ED-25; LinkedIn automation is ED-23; two-step posts and content-driven inbound DMs are ED-24.

## Purpose
Book sales calls from social direct messages, without ads, by starting human conversations
and letting the prospect ask for help.

## When to use
Outbound prospecting, especially for a new agency with few case studies.

## Inputs
Profiles in the target niche (ED-05), the "problems you solve" list, the sales-call workflow.

## Steps (his order)
0. **The outbound loop** (S-14 · lesson 43 "30,000ft View Of Prospecting Outbound"): get a list
   (ED-25) → pick **one** platform, the one where you have the most contacts → message as a
   person → log every conversation on a tracking board, at least **25 a day**
   `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14` → book calls. Run it alongside paid inbound (ED-02) so you
   are not waiting on ads. Consistency is the method; he stresses it is not complicated.
1. **Open** — spend about half a minute on the profile; open on something you share, as a person.
   He sets a daily volume of **25 new messages** `[tag: assumption] stated by S-13`.
   For someone you already know (email or phone contacts), reopen from a past conversation
   instead (S-14 · lesson 51 "How To Book Calls From Chat/DM/Text Organically").
2. **Warm up** when they reply — engage with their posts and stories, compliment, then ask
   about their business and the problems your service solves.
3. **Indirect ask** — do not pitch. Ask if they know someone in their niche with that
   problem, and mention you are opening a few spots. They either refer someone or raise
   their own hand.
4. **Offer by stage**:
   - no experience yet → an introductory service for free;
   - some experience, short of clients → a discount (he gives 30%, 40% or 50% as options
     `[tag: assumption] stated by S-13`; the video widens it to 20–80%
     `[tag: conflict]` S-13 vs S-14 · lesson 51).
   In exchange: they use the videos, give feedback and a review or case study.
5. **Book** a short fit call in the next couple of days; run the sales call (ED-07). Do not try
   to close over text. Booking mechanics (S-14 · lesson 52 "How To Book The Call Using Your
   Calendar"): send the calendar's booking link so the lead lands in the CRM with a meeting
   link; if they keep not booking, ask for an email and a time and book them yourself
   (S-14 · lesson 57 "Evan Messaging Review + 2 Step Post Templates"). Following up two or
   three times to get a booking is normal, not a sign of disinterest (lesson 57).
6. **Earn the call before offering it** (S-14 · lesson 57): with an *outbound* or lukewarm
   contact, dig into the problem, where they are, where they want to be and the gap before you
   offer a call — the call should be framed as the answer to a problem they have named. Go
   straight to a call only when the contact came *inbound* expecting one. Some contacts are
   only ready to network: frame it that way and put them in long-term follow-up.
7. **Follow up and handle objections** (S-14 · lesson 53 "Follow Up + Objection Handling
   Frameworks (DM or Text)"): a set of "ghost" follow-ups for no reply and short objection
   replies, kept as a document; the video only points to it.
8. **Upsell after the free or discounted job** (S-14 · lesson 54 "How To Upsell Client After
   Case Study", a role-play), in **one call**: record the case study, stop recording, thank
   them, then ask where they want to go from here. Then:
   1. show the packages (ED-05) and ask which they lean towards;
   2. **break-even arithmetic** — price ÷ the value of one of their clients = clients needed to
      pay it back; ask whether the proposed volume of content could plausibly bring that many;
   3. **cost of inaction** — ask what continuing as they are leaves on the table;
   4. objections: *time* → spread their on-camera time across weeks; *cash flow* → offer a
      monthly split and start with the deliverable they named as most urgent; *fear of being
      locked in* → a short "love it or leave it" first period or the shortest tier (ED-05);
      billing runs on a rolling 30 days from the start date;
   5. when they say yes, ask how they want to pay and **stop talking** — extra words give them
      room to reconsider.

## Decision points
- They refuse even the free offer → ask why; the real cost is usually their time, so
  remove it (bring the ideas, post for them, ask nothing of them).
- After a free job → case study at once → ask for feedback → offer the paid packages at a discount.

## Outputs
Booked calls; early case studies and reviews.

## Open edges
- Free and discounted work is an entry tactic, not a business model; he gives no exit rule
  for how many free clients to take (H-15).
- His follow-up and objection frameworks for DMs: the video (lesson 53) is a 21-second pointer to
  a document, so the frameworks themselves are in that document, not the video (narrows H-11).
- **Reciprocity is used as leverage.** He says free work makes the prospect feel they owe you
  and tells students to lean on that. Our version: free or discounted work is an honest trade
  stated up front (video and feedback in return), never a debt to collect.
- **Open tracking** (lesson 55) puts a tracking pixel in one-to-one email. Under GDPR/ePrivacy
  (EU/UK) that generally needs consent; disclose it or do not use it for those recipients.
- **Unsolicited messages:** DMs to people you do not know must follow each platform's rules on
  volume and unsolicited contact; texting contacts who have not opted in risks TCPA (US) and
  similar laws (see ED-09); emailing a list you exported must follow CAN-SPAM (US: real
  identity, physical address, working opt-out) and, for EU/UK recipients, GDPR/PECR.
- The upsell role-play cites a "money left on the table" close borrowed from another speaker;
  as a question it is fine, as pressure it is not.
