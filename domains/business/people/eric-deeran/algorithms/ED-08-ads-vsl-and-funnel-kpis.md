# ED-08 — Ad and VSL structure, opt-in friction, and weekly funnel KPIs

`source: stated` (S-13, sections "Creating Your VSL & Ads" and "Setting Up Ads") unless marked.
Items marked **S-14** come from the course videos of the same sections (cited as
`S-14 · lesson <n> "<title>"`). Script *structure* only; no lines copied. The full scripting
method is ED-17; the campaign build and decision tree are ED-16.

## Purpose
The creative and measurement layer under ED-02: what the ad and the video sales letter (VSL)
say, in what order, how to raise lead quality, and which numbers to check each week.

## When to use
Building or fixing the paid funnel.

## Inputs
The positioning line and offer (ED-05), 4–5 named pillars (ED-07), past client results,
the ad account and CRM.

## Steps
1. **Choose the ad type** (S-13 · "2 Types Of Video Ad Scripts"):
   - **Offer-forward** (≤ 30–60 s) — use when you have strong results and a guarantee.
     Order: hook calling out the buyer → their pain and the outcome they want → the offer and
     guarantee → why it works (past results) → watch the short video.
     Fewer, more problem-aware leads, each costing more.
   - **Indirect** (≤ 60 s) — a free system or training in exchange for contact details.
     Order: hook as a question → three pains and what they cost → "others felt the same" →
     the free system → an honest why (we earn your trust; you can also do it yourself) →
     where to send it. More, cheaper leads; more freebie-seekers.
   Lengths `[tag: assumption] stated by S-13`.
   **S-14** (lesson 192) confirms both scripts and adds: in the indirect script you pitch the
   free thing, never your service; script and test both. Before choosing, pick the lead
   magnet by buyer stage — free resource, case study or VSL (lesson 166; ED-17 step 1) — and
   write a hook, body and call to action for each funnel stage (lesson 191; ED-17 step 5).
   **S-14** (lesson 227): the ad matters more than the VSL; a failed VSL test can be fixed by
   new ads alone `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14, one test`.
2. **Write the VSL** (S-13 · "VSL Full Example + Script"), sections in order:
   opening (who is speaking) → direct call-out of the audience and the outcome → erase doubts
   (feel–felt–found) → reframe a common belief (people buy from who they know and trust, not
   the best product) → reveal the named steps of your system → social proof → one or two
   sentences per step → the offer, the guarantee, and a call to book a fit call.
   **S-14** (lesson 179) adds the reasons: give the method a name the buyer has never heard,
   so they hold no prior opinion of it; the reframe tells them what matters; the tutorial
   teaches "the what, not the how" so they see the work and want help. Keep something
   changing on screen about every 10 seconds (lesson 175) `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`.
   S-13 places social proof after the steps; the video example and most member VSLs
   (lessons 181–190) weave testimonial clips between the steps `[tag: conflict]` (minor:
   placement, not content).
3. **Add friction to the opt-in, one question at a time** (S-13 · "Lead Quality Not Great?"),
   least to most sales-like: owner of a business in the niche? → ready to invest in content
   that brings customers? → how soon? → monthly revenue band. Disqualifiers: not the owner;
   not ready to invest; "a couple of months"; revenue under about $20k a month — the revenue
   line moves with your price `[tag: assumption] stated by S-13`.
   Add the next question only after watching lead volume.
   **S-14** (lesson 236) confirms the order and the one-at-a-time rule (so a drop in volume
   points at one question). Lesson 233 adds a single **conditional-logic** gate at the start
   of the instant form: buyers whose monthly revenue is about ten times your monthly price
   continue, everyone else lands on a "not a fit" screen `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`.
4. **Track weekly** (S-13 · "Fill Out Data Tracking Sheet") — four weekly tabs, three blocks:
   advertising → funnel → sales. Look at the month too when the sales cycle is long.
   **S-14** (lesson 248) names a fourth block, **profitability** (revenue ÷ ad spend; revenue,
   not cash collected), and states the purpose: bring the sheet to coaching so decisions are
   made from data, and the weakest stage is found by comparing each number with its target.
5. **Attribute lead quality to the creative** — **S-14** (lesson 238): after each triage
   call, note the lead's fit in a fixed format in the CRM, then look up which ad they came
   from; over 1–2 weeks keep the creatives that bring qualified people and cut the ones that
   do not, even if their leads are cheaper. Manual, not a tool. Closes H-13.
6. **Decide with the tree** — **S-14** (lesson 237): no leads → wait until about $60 is spent,
   then change the creative; high cost per click or low click-through → the ad; clicks but no
   opt-ins → the page; unqualified leads → friction (step 3); qualified but unsold → sales
   (ED-07). Full tree in ED-16 step 7.

## Decision points
- Strong proof and guarantee → offer-forward; need volume or cheaper leads → indirect.
- Lead quality poor → add the next friction question; lead flow collapses → remove it.
- **S-14**: a creative with cheap leads that never qualify loses to a dearer one that closes
  (lesson 238). A small client who needs volume now → embed the calendar under the video
  and drop the separate application step (lesson 343).

## Outputs
An ad, a VSL, an opt-in form and a weekly scorecard.
