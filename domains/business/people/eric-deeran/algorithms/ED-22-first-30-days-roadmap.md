# ED-22 — The first 30 days: onboarding, daily tracking, proof, mindset and foundations

`source: stated` (S-14, sections "Welcome Video & Onboarding Sheet (Do This First!)" and
"Business Fundamentals") unless marked.

## Purpose
What he has a new student do before and during the first weeks: arrive at the kickoff with
the facts written down, work one week-by-week checklist, track acquisition every day, collect
proof from past clients, understand the one pipeline the program builds and where it usually
breaks, set the right expectations about volume, and put the legal and insurance basics in
place.

## When to use
Day 0 to roughly week 4 of a new student (or a new client of a coaching or done-with-you
program). The shape transfers to our own course start.

## Inputs
The onboarding sheet, a project board with the week-by-week checklist, a private chat channel
with the coaches, an acquisition tracking sheet, a list of past clients.

## Steps
1. **Fill the onboarding sheet before the kickoff call** (S-14 · lesson 5 "Please Fill Out Your
   Onboarding Sheet Here Immediately"); the kickoff sets up the private chat channel and the
   project board. Install the course portal, the chat app and the board app on your phone so no
   message is missed (lesson 3 "Mobile App Access").
2. **Work the board week by week** (lesson 6 "How To Use Your Client Start Up Checklist"; lesson 2
   "Time Management: Common Pitfalls"). The board is in order: week 1, week 2, week 3 (outbound lead
   generation), then ongoing items. **Mark each item done on the day you do it** — the board stamps
   the date, so coaches can see who is behind or ahead and steer. The board is for status only, not
   messages; all communication goes through chat. His time rule: it is about priorities, not
   available time — and "you can't be busy and broke at the same time".
3. **Answer your own question first** (lesson 4 "What To Do First When You Have Questions"): (a) is
   it the next step on the board? (b) search the portal and the chat — there is probably a short
   training on it, edited tight, which is faster than a call; (c) only then post or book a 1:1.
4. **Use the chat channels by topic** (lesson 7 "How To Use Your Slack Channel"): a private channel
   with your coaches, plus shared channels for sales, scaling and teams, offers and pricing,
   marketing and ads, general, the coaching-call schedule and recordings, and wins. It is the only
   channel of communication, so everything stays in one place.
5. **Post every win, including small ones** (lesson 8 "How To Use The #Wins Channel") — a finished
   offer sheet, ads going live, the first lead. Celebrating small progress keeps attention on what is
   working; wins also feed a weekly leaderboard.
6. **Track acquisition every day** (lesson 6) on one sheet, by the student and later by their
   salesperson: conversations had, sales calls booked, shown, closed, revenue sold, cash collected,
   day completed. Minimum **25 conversations a day, goal 50** `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`.
   Coaches check the sheet.
7. **Collect proof from past clients** (lesson 15 "Getting Previous Client Case Studies"; lesson 19
   "Self Guided Case Study"). List every past client; re-approach each with a request to *feature*
   them and get their feedback (not a request for a favour); record a short video call using the
   case-study script, even if the result was only "they were great to work with". If a client will
   not appear, write a self-guided case study recapping what you did. The three example interviews
   (lessons 16–18, students' own clients) share one order, `source: derived` from them: who the
   client is → the problem before → why they chose this agency → what the process felt like →
   the result → what changed for the owner personally → advice to someone on the fence → a call to
   book.
8. **Understand the one pipeline and diagnose by stage** (lesson 9 "The 30,000ft View Process";
   lesson 10 "Theory Of Constraints & Growing Your Agency"). Traffic (an ad with the offer "we help
   this type of business get this result with our content") → a page that captures name, email and
   phone → a value video that pitches for you → a calendar with qualifying questions → a booked call
   → a thank-you page with objection-handling content → the call. Every lead enters the CRM for
   automated follow-up; contact a new lead **within 5–10 minutes**; most bookings come from follow-up,
   not first visit (detail in ED-02). His diagnosis map, one bottleneck at a time:
   | Symptom | Likely broken stage |
   | --- | --- |
   | no traffic | the offer or the ad |
   | traffic but bad leads | offer, ad message or targeting misaligned |
   | leads but no calls | the value video or its message |
   | calls but no closes | the sales process |
   His default for video agencies: the bottleneck is **leads, not delivery** — clients are happy, there
   are just not enough of them, so time spent polishing delivery does not grow the business.
   Referrals are good but not repeatable; a pipeline is, because you can pour more in at the top.
9. **Set the mindset** (lessons 11–14):
   - **technician vs owner** — a technician (shooter, editor) owns a job and is capped at their own
     hours; an owner owns a system and buys other people's hours. He frames it with a well-known
     four-quadrant cash-flow model (employee → self-employed → business owner);
   - ask **"how can I make this work for me?"**, not "will this work for me?" — the first looks for
     gaps, the second for reasons it fails (his skier-between-trees analogy); success is the student's
     own responsibility;
   - **done is better than perfect** — ship a minimum version of the offer sheet, ads, VSL, page and
     case studies, then iterate on feedback;
   - **expect more volume than you think**: 50–100 outreach messages a day, $25–100 a day in ad spend
     and enough leads before judging a campaign; he says he made 15–20 ads before one worked and ran it
     weeks to months (all `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`). His rule: "If you don't quit, you don't
     fail."
10. **Put the foundations in place** (S-14 · lessons 25–31; detail in ED-13): company registration,
    tax ID and business bank account; a bank merchant account; retainers on auto-pay; general and
    professional liability insurance (he suggests ~$1M cover, an annual policy, customer-property
    cover) plus separate equipment insurance at replacement value with theft cover, and **every
    contractor on set shows their own insurance first**; a savings habit from the first month.

## Decision points
- Behind on the board or overwhelmed → say so in chat; coaches cannot help with what they do not
  see (lesson 2).
- A past client will not record → self-guided case study (lesson 19).
- "It didn't work for me" → find the stage in the table above before changing the whole system.

## Outputs
A filled onboarding sheet, a board with dated completions, a daily acquisition sheet, a first set
of case studies, and the basic legal, payment and insurance setup.

## His stated figures (all `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`; falsifier: our own cohort data)
- Lead → booked call **15–30%**; booked call → close **20–40%** "depending on price"; clients worth
  **$5–10K** each. His worked example — 100 leads → 15–30 calls → 3–6 clients → $30–60K — does not
  multiply out from his own ranges (100 × 15–30% × 20–40% = 3–12 clients; × $5–10K = $15–120K)
  `[tag: conflict]`. Follow-up produces **~80%** of bookings, self-booking ~20%. Show and close rates
  also conflict with ED-07, ED-08, ED-11 (H-12).
- Program results: the onboarding video says the program can "guarantee" a result if the board is
  followed on time, because completion is tracked; pipeline screenshots claim e.g. ~$317K and ~$73K
  closed in about two months from the inbound system; the example testimonials claim ~4× return on
  ~$40K of ad spend, millions of views per ad and 60–70% yearly growth. None is verifiable here; the
  clients are not named in this file. The "four years" of training content (lesson 4) is likewise his
  statement.

## Open edges
- **Guarantee language.** A "guaranteed result" tied to completing a checklist shifts the risk onto
  the student; any guarantee we make needs a written results definition, a time limit and the
  conditions stated up front (as H-15).
- **Testimonial rules.** Case studies with income or ROI figures need consent, honest context and, in
  the US, a clear statement of typical results under endorsement rules; keep the recordings and the
  client's written permission.
- **Responsibility framing.** "How can I make this work?" is useful for a student and can also be used
  to blame the student when the program fails; pair it with the program's own published completion and
  result data (proposed H-19).
- Insurance quotes are one provider, one state, one date; requirements differ by country and by what
  is filmed.
- What the onboarding sheet asks, and the case-study script itself, are linked documents not in the
  videos (S-13 may hold them).
