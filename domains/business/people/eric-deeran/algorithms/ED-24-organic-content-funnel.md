# ED-24 — Organic content funnel (uniform profiles → five content types on a cycle → call-to-action posts → DM or lead page → call)

`source: stated` (S-14, section "Creating An Organic Content Funnel", lessons 207–224; the
two-step post lessons 56–57 from "Prospecting") unless marked. Lessons 213–224 are short
example clips; we describe what each example is *for*, not what it says. Lesson 256
"EX. Quote Cards" is a silent example with no transcript.

## Purpose
Use free social content to (a) prove you are real to people who saw your ad or message and
went to check your profile, and (b) generate leads on its own by turning viewers into
comments, DMs and opt-ins.

## When to use
From the start, beside paid ads (ED-02) and outbound (ED-06, ED-23). He calls it slower than
paid: expect weeks without traction before it compounds.

## Inputs
The offer statement (ED-05), a lead capture page or VSL, a free resource worth asking for, a
scheduling tool, optionally a comment-automation tool, recorded footage (batched).

## Steps (his order)
1. **Know the job of the content** (S-14 · lesson 208 "How An Organic Content Funnel Works"):
   a prospect who sees an ad checks your profile; the feed must answer "is this person
   legit?" with proof and teaching, then point back to booking. It can also feed ads
   ("fire or fuel") or stand alone (lesson 207 "Why You Need An Organic Content Funnel").
2. **Make every profile say the same thing** (S-14 · lesson 209 "How To Optimize Your Profile
   On All Platforms"): a bio that calls out the target audience and what you do for them, the
   lead-capture link in the bio, a professional picture, a header image, contact info filled
   in — **uniform across all platforms**.
3. **Pick five content types and cycle them** (lesson 208). His menu:
   - quote cards (lesson 256, silent example);
   - previous client testimonials — the client explains, in their words, what the work made
     possible (example: lesson 213);
   - behind-the-scenes clips from shoots, usually set to a trending sound (lessons 214–215);
   - finished videos made for clients;
   - repurposed content (he once recorded himself on a sales call and reused it);
   - **educational talking head — "the bulk of what we do"**. His examples (lessons 216–219)
     show four patterns: answer a question buyers ask (why retainers beat one-off projects);
     a reverse-psychology hook ("don't do X if you don't want to grow"); an insider tip that
     helps buyers choose a provider; and a one-idea case for a single deliverable (a short
     brand story film);
   - trends — trending formats and sounds (lessons 220–221);
   - call-to-action posts (step 5).
4. **Batch and schedule** (S-14 · lessons 208, 210 "How To Schedule All Posts Via Metricool"):
   record in batches, edit (or hand to an editor), then schedule everything in one or two days
   at the start of the month through a scheduler that posts to every platform at once, using
   its suggested best times; at least one post a day. Watch growth per platform in the same
   tool's analytics.
5. **Mix in call-to-action posts** (lesson 208). Three kinds, and they attract different
   buyers:
   - **link in bio** — "a short video on how we do X is in my bio" (example: lesson 222);
   - **DM for a free resource** — "DM me the word *content* and I'll send the list"
     (example: lesson 223);
   - **direct "I'm looking for three…"** — a stated number of a stated kind of business, a
     qualifier ("only serious, ready to invest"), DM a keyword (example: lesson 224).
   He says the direct one brings the most qualified buyers (closest to acting); the other two
   bring people still gathering information, who need the other content to nurture them.
6. **Two-step post** (S-14 · lesson 56 "What Is A 2-Step Post + How To Use It To Generate
   Leads"; lesson 57): a post that names one niche's pain and offers a free training on it —
   "comment below and we'll send it". Comments push the post up the feed and draw more
   comments (a crowd effect). Reply publicly to each commenter, friend or connect with them,
   DM the training, then ask what made them want it and how business is going, and continue
   with ED-06. It works because the prospect asked for something from you, so you write from
   authority instead of as a stranger. Prerequisites: you have friended people in the niche
   (ED-25) so they see it, and you have short trainings on your process ready. The more
   specific the niche, the better it pulls.
7. **Automate the comment-to-DM step** (S-14 · lesson 211 "How To Use ManyChat To Automate
   Comments"): a trigger on a keyword in a comment (all posts) or in a DM; a public reply drawn
   at random from a few short variants ("sent", "check your DMs") so it does not read as a bot;
   a DM with the opt-in link (name, email, phone) to the VSL. The reply comments also lift the
   post. The tool reports leads per keyword. The same set-up can be sold to clients.
8. **Route the lead down one of two paths** (lesson 208): link in bio → lead capture → value
   video → book → sales call; or DM → qualify in the DM → sales call (ED-06, ED-07).

## Decision points
- Low traction early → keep going; he reports about two months with none at first.
- Choose the CTA by buyer stage: direct for the ready, resource or bio link for the curious.
- An inbound DM from content can go straight to a call; a lukewarm commenter needs the
  problem dug out first (ED-06 step 6).

## Outputs
A consistent profile on every platform, a month of scheduled posts, keyword-triggered DMs,
opt-ins and booked calls.

## Open edges
- **Testimonials and endorsements** must be genuine, from real clients, and not edited to
  claim results they did not get (FTC Endorsement Guides in the US; similar rules elsewhere).
- **Repurposing a recorded sales call** needs the other party's consent to record and to
  publish; some US states and many countries require all-party consent.
- **"I'm looking for three…"** is a scarcity claim; it must be true (a real capacity limit),
  or it is a misleading sales claim.
- **Comment and DM automation** must stay inside the platform's messaging rules (on Meta, the
  automated reply is tied to the user's own comment or message and its response window); a
  keyword opt-in that then sends texts needs SMS consent (ED-09, TCPA).
- Nothing in S-14 measures which content type or CTA produced a booked call; "five types" and
  "direct CTA is most qualified" are untested by us (held open).
- Quote cards (lesson 256) are shown without commentary.

## Canon map
`source: derived`. **Brand and Identity:** step 2 is *consistency across touchpoints* (one
statement, one link, every platform); the bio's "who + outcome" and the two-step post's named
pain are the *gradient* from the viewer's current state to the promised state; the cycle of
five types at a fixed daily rhythm is *frequency matched to audience*, and "quality followers
over many" is the audience half of that match; the purpose is set before the content (step 1).
**ACE Acquisition:** each platform and each CTA keyword is an attention channel; the bio link
and keyword DMs *centralize* them into one opt-in; the comment automation is *automate first
response*; the tool's leads-per-keyword count is the seed of *track ROI per channel* — add a
source tag to each opt-in so content-sourced calls can be compared with ads and outbound and
effort reallocated. For a beginner affiliate marketer the same shape runs in a free social
planner: uniform profile, five post types on a schedule, a weekly two-step post, keyword DMs to
one link.
