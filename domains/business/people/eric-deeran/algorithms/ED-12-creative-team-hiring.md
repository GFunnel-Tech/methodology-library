# ED-12 — Creative team hiring: editors, videographers, producers

`source: stated` (S-13, section "Hiring/Managing/Leading Your Team"; S-14, the same
section's video lessons 258–267) unless marked.

## Purpose
Move delivery off the founder with contract editors, videographers and a producer, without
losing margin — and run their work through one file structure, one review loop and one
daily report.

## When to use
When fulfillment, not sales, is the constraint (ED-01). In his sequence the editor is the
first hire of the whole business and the videographer the second (ED-21).

## Inputs
A portfolio of finished work to set the standard, a contractor agreement, a training doc,
shared storage, a video review tool, a team chat.

## Steps (his order)
1. **Source, per role**:
   - editor (S-13 · "How To Hire A Video Editor"; S-14 · lesson 259 "How To Hire A Video
     Editor") — he calls it "the Facebook method": join large editor communities on Facebook,
     post the job, compare submissions on quality for the price. **The application is the
     test**: candidates receive a finished edit as the reference plus the raw clips, and
     re-create it; anyone unwilling is filtered out. He prefers editors in lower-cost countries
     to protect margin.
   - videographer (S-14 · lesson 264 "How To Hire A Videographer") — search Instagram for the
     role filtered to your city, screen portfolios for the style you need, then follow and
     message the good ones about a specific shoot; optionally run an automated DM campaign
     that sends people to an application survey in the CRM.
   - producer (S-14 · lesson 266 "How To Hire A Producer") — search LinkedIn for producers in
     your city; hand-pick and message, or drive an automated DM campaign to a survey and build
     a candidate pipeline.
2. **Interview in stages** (S-13 SOPs; S-14 · lessons 260, 265, 267): screen the portfolio →
   round 1 on basics → round 2 on depth → evaluation (portfolio again in light of the answers,
   team fit) → written offer (pay, scope, start) → tell unsuccessful candidates promptly. For
   the producer he brings a team member into round 1 for a second opinion, asks follow-ups in
   round 2, then decides with the team.
   What each role is tested on:
   - editor: software, deadlines, organising footage, colour, audio, formats, taking feedback;
   - videographer: equipment, difficult conditions, lighting, matching a director's vision,
     shoot planning, backups;
   - producer: leading a team, budgets and schedules, conflict, pre-/production/post
     workflow, vendors and talent, several projects at once.
3. **Sign a contractor agreement with confidentiality** (S-13; S-14 · lesson 258 "Independent
   Contractor Agreement & NDA") — one template for any 1099 contractor (sales or creative),
   brackets filled per hire, signed before they start; tax form collected through the payroll
   service. He says to have a lawyer review it. Terms as in S-13: scope, dates, fixed fee and
   schedule, contractor status, confidentiality during and after, return of materials, company
   owns the work, 30 days' notice. **On set, every contractor must carry and show their own
   liability insurance** (S-14 · lesson 29 "Business Insurance"; see ED-22).
4. **Train** — a written or recorded process (short screen recordings) for importing, editing,
   colour, captions, export and hand-in for review, and which storage and review tools to use.
5. **Give every project the same file structure** (S-14 · lesson 261 "Editors File Structure &
   Storage") — a shared cloud drive synced to the desktop; one folder per year, one template
   project copied per job and named by client; inside: project files, media, audio, graphics,
   final exports, documents. Editors are added to the folder and ping when an export lands.
6. **Run revisions in two loops** (S-14 · lesson 262 "Editors: How We Do Revisions For Internal &
   External Content") — the cut goes to a video review tool with time-coded notes. *Internal
   loop:* the account manager (or the founder) reviews and returns notes to the editor until it
   passes quality control. *External loop:* only then does the client see it; the client's notes
   come back through the account manager, who relays them to the editor. **The client never
   sees a draft that has not passed internal QC**, and the editor never talks to the client
   directly.
7. **End-of-day report for every creative** (S-14 · lesson 263 "EOD Reporting For Editors") — a
   short form posted to the team's end-of-day channel: name, hours worked, videos completed and
   their state, how the day went, where they need help, notes. Adapt it for shooters (hours on
   set) and producers.

## His stated pay (`[tag: assumption] stated by S-13 / S-14`)
Editors $10–20 per short reel (edit, colour, captions), $100–200 per long-form edit (brand
film, case study); paid per piece through a low-fee payment app, booked as contractor
expense. In the time-audit lesson an in-country editor is costed at **$25–30/hour** (S-14 ·
lesson 23 "Why Is A Time Audit Important") — a different hiring mode, not a contradiction.
His payroll example shows an "independent contractor" editor on a **$2,000/month base**
(S-14 · lesson 284 "How To Track Payroll & Payroll Periods") `[tag: conflict]` with per-piece
pay — and see the classification note below.

## Outputs
A small bench of trained contractors per role, working to one folder template, one review
loop and one daily report.

## Sources
S-13 (hiring guide, three interview SOPs, a second producer question bank, contractor
agreement). S-14 · lessons 258–267, 23, 29, 284.

## Open edges
- **Unpaid trial work.** The editor application asks candidates to edit real footage for free.
  Use dummy or already-published footage, keep it short, and consider paying for it; using a
  test edit in client work without paying for it is not acceptable (proposed H-18).
- **Classification.** A monthly base, set file structures, daily reports and required tools
  are signs of employment in many jurisdictions; per-piece pay with the contractor's own
  methods is the safer pattern (proposed H-17).
- **Offshore pay.** "We pay less because the currency is worth more" is a margin choice; check
  local tax and payment rules for the contractor's country, and state rates in writing.
- The contractor agreement still has no non-solicit or IP-assignment clause beyond ownership; a
  lawyer should complete it.
- H-11 is closed for file structure, revisions and the editor end-of-day report.
