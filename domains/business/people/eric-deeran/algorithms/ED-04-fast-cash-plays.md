# ED-04 — Fast-cash plays (reactivation · Ghost Proposal · free video to upsell)

`source: stated` (S-13, course section "Reactivation / Fast Cash Strategy"; S-14, the same
section's three videos, lessons 020–022) unless marked. Restated in our words; no course text
copied. Additions from the videos are marked **S-14**.

## Purpose
Three ways to get sales calls in days, with no ad spend, while the paid system (ED-02) is
being built. All three end in the same place: the sales call (ED-07).

## When to use
Early in a student's programme, or any time cash is short and there is an existing list,
an audience, or a portfolio to lean on.

## Inputs
A list of past clients and warm leads; a social following on at least one platform; a
"problems you solve" list (ED-07); the sales-call workflow; for play C, the ability to
deliver one excellent video for free.

## Play A — Reactivate past clients and warm leads (S-13 · "Reactivating Past Clients/Warm Leads")
1. Contact every past client and warm lead by email, text or phone with a short note that
   something new has launched that may help them, and ask for a chat in the next few days.
2. Book the call as soon as possible (the delay is what causes no-shows) and send a
   calendar invite and video link.
3. On the call: rapport → re-open discovery → diagnose → walk through the pitch → close on
   a new **retainer** offer.
4. **Decision:** no reply → follow up, about 2–3 days apart, changing channel
   (email → call → text). He sets a minimum of **5 follow-ups** `[tag: assumption] stated by S-13`
   (repeated in S-14 · lesson 020). His stance: silence usually means busy, not no.
- **S-14 adds** (S-14 · lesson 020 "Reactivating Past Clients/Warm Leads"):
  - no CRM yet → first write every past client into a list with name, phone and email;
  - the opener deliberately **does not say what is new** — curiosity is what gets them to the
    call. It may name the pain they raised last time. The core line is the method, so we keep
    its gist: *"we just rolled something new out that might help you — do you have time to
    chat in the next couple of days?"*;
  - book the call as soon as possible after the reply, to cut no-shows;
  - the follow-up messages are written to sound **busy, not desperate** (a note between
    meetings, a note on leaving the office), ending in a short "where should we go from
    here?" as the last one;
  - the call closes them on the new **retainer** pricing, not the old project price;
  - he frames the play as the programme's quick first win so students have cash early.

## Play B — The Ghost Proposal Method (S-13 · "The Ghost Proposal Method™")
1. Post on Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn: open with your credibility, say you are
   thinking of making a free resource on an outcome tied to your offer, and ask people to
   comment for early access.
2. Message everyone who comments. Run three phases:
   - **Problem** — frame it as research to make the resource better; ask about their
     problems using your "problems you solve" list.
   - **Cost** — how long it has gone on, whether it costs them clients, what a client is
     worth; multiply into a dollar size for the problem.
   - **Pitch call** — ask whether a short call would be worth it if you could fix it.
3. Run the sales call and close.
- Decision point: the comment is the only gate; every commenter gets a message.
- **S-14 adds** (S-14 · lesson 021 "The Ghost Proposal Method"):
  - **its stated purpose is market research**: test which angle people raise their hand for,
    before building anything. Commenters "pay with their time" first;
  - **post on all three platforms** with the structure *background → social proof → the
    resource you are thinking of making → comment for early access*;
  - **no comments → change the angle and post again**; keep testing until one lands, then
    double down. He names giving up after one post as the common failure;
  - the winning angle becomes the lead magnet and the angle of the VSL and ads in the
    inbound system (ED-02, ED-08);
  - small network → grow it first (friend target clients, run LinkedIn connection outreach);
  - the DM questions come from the "problems you solve" list; the cost phase gets the prospect
    to put a dollar figure on the problem themselves; the pitch-call line is a simple "if we
    could fix that, would a short chat be worth it?";
  - he states that people on the call **often forget about the resource**
    `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14; see open edges`.

## Play C — Free video to the right business, then upsell (S-13 · "How [a member] Closed … From Offering 3 'Free' Videos")
1. Define the ideal client and find matching businesses online.
2. Screen hard: they can pay, they have a good product and happy customers, and they
   genuinely need more clients.
3. Call; tell the gatekeeper you are offering one free piece of content to a local business
   with no strings attached; get the decision-maker's email.
4. Email the decision-maker: who you are, why their business was chosen, links to your
   work, a small time ask (a half-day on site), delivery within about two weeks, and an
   explicit "no catch".
5. Deliver work as good as for a paying client — he says the play fails otherwise.
6. **Wait for them to ask the price**, then upsell.
7. After they get results, record a case study.
- **S-14 adds** (S-14 · lesson 022, an interview with a member about this play):
  - **screen for a business that can pay** — a visible presence and budget, not the smallest
    shop; ask around about them;
  - the gatekeeper call gives a reason for the offer (in the example, being new to the area);
    getting the decision-maker's email was easy because nothing was being sold;
  - the email states the **time cost to the client** (a block of a few hours) and **when they
    get it** (about two weeks), because free still costs them time and disruption; he would
    add the result they could expect from it;
  - **no fake invoice** showing a discounted price — he has tried it and says it undercuts
    the "no strings" message;
  - **wait for them to ask the price**; asking first spends the goodwill built by giving.
    In the example the client asked what it would have cost; the member asked what they
    would be comfortable investing, then offered three packages and the client chose the
    middle one `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14; one case`;
  - **only do a couple of these** — once you have proof in a niche, stop; the member planned
    one every six months, aimed at a carefully chosen business;
  - the principle he draws: free work must be as good as paid work, or it proves the
    opposite of what you want.

## Decision points
- Which play: list exists → A; audience exists → B; portfolio and time but no audience → C.

## Outputs
Booked sales calls; for C, a case study as well as a client.

## Open edges
- Play B promises a resource the documents never say to produce. If it is never made, the
  post misleads the commenters. Our version must make the resource. (H-15)
  `[tag: conflict]` S-14 lesson 021 is explicit that the resource need not exist yet and
  that callers "forget about it"; it also says the winning post's resource is later built
  for the inbound system. So the losing angles' commenters are never given what they asked
  for. Our version: build the resource for every angle that got comments, or promise only
  what exists.
- **Reactivation by text or call needs a lawful basis.** Past clients and old leads may not
  have consented to marketing texts; cold-texting a list from the CRM risks carrier flags
  (the course's own warning: an opt-out rate above about 2% gets the account flagged,
  S-14 · lesson 159) and consent law. Email and a personal call to people you actually worked
  with are the safer channels; texts only to contacts with recorded SMS consent (ED-09).
- **"Rolled something new out" must be true.** The curiosity opener works only if there is a
  new offer; used as a pretext it is a small deception.
- Play C's "no catch" email and the later upsell are in tension; see H-15.
- The videos for these lessons are now transcribed (S-14, lessons 020–022); H-11 is closed
  for this section.

## Canon map
ACE Acquisition with zero spend. Play A is ED-P01 move 7 made concrete. Play B's
Problem → Cost → Pitch-call order resembles the ACE conversation (pain, then its price,
then the ask). Play B is also a cheap test of **ROI per channel and angle** before spending
on ads — the canon's Acquisition measure, run on comments instead of cost. Play C's
"case study after results" feeds ACE Expansion's proof and referral loop. Reactivation of
past clients sits between Expansion (existing clients) and Acquisition (warm leads); the
canon's *document everything in the CRM* step is what makes the list exist to reactivate.
