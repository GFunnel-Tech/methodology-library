# ED-11 — Sales team operating system: when to hire, pay, run the day, forecast

`source: stated` (S-13, section "Hiring/Managing/Leading Your Team" and the sales dashboard;
S-14, the same section's 26 video lessons) unless marked.

## Purpose
Take the founder off the sales calls: hire setters and closers only when a constraint
calls for it, pay them on results, run them with two short daily meetings and a fortnightly
1:1, see their day at a glance, and forecast the calls needed to hit a target.

## When to use
Once the funnel books more calls than the founder can take (ED-P01 move 9) — and only once
the prerequisites in step 1 hold. The order of hires across the whole business is ED-21.

## Inputs
A working funnel (ED-02/ED-08), the sales dashboard and tracking sheet, the CRM with
separate setter and closer pipelines, a team chat, shared calendars.

## Steps
1. **Check the hire is earned** (S-14 · lesson 268 "When To Hire Setters"; lesson 269 "When
   To Hire Closers"). Every hire must remove a current or near-future constraint; a hire
   with no constraint behind it only enlarges the team and cuts margin. He hires a **setter
   first, a closer last**.
   - *Setter* — solves two things: too few booked calls from the leads already coming in,
     and poor money-per-lead (by calling and texting each lead for days, a setter books more
     calls from the same ad spend). Prerequisites: the offer has been sold consistently by
     the founder (**5–10 clients** `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`); a steady lead flow of
     **500–800 leads a month per setter** `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`, inbound or warm,
     never cold lists (he says setters do not survive cold outbound); delivery is not already
     the bottleneck; the founder has built the acquisition system first.
   - *Closer* — hired only when the founder's calendar is full of sales calls. Prerequisites:
     the founder has closed the offer **5–10 times at ≥25%** `[tag: assumption] stated by
     S-14`; the company can supply **≥3 calls a day (15 a week)** `[tag: assumption] stated by
     S-14`; the offer is priced high enough for commission to be worth it (**≥$10K** `[tag:
     assumption] stated by S-14`); delivery is already delegated. The founder's own recorded
     calls become the closer's training library, and the founder's close rate becomes the
     baseline that shows when a rep is underperforming.
   - Hiring too early, in his account: reps doubt the offer, there is no baseline to coach
     against, reps quit for lack of calls, and only weak candidates apply.
2. **Source** (S-13 · "How To Hire An Appointment Setter/Closer"; S-14 · lesson 272 "How To
   Hire An Appointment Setter/Closer"; lesson 273 "Hiring Appointment Setter/Close – Using A
   Survey & Website") — he skips paid staffing agencies (you still have to train the rep) and
   posts in high-ticket sales groups on Facebook. Applicants email a résumé plus a 30–60
   second self-introduction video, copied to a second team member; the video lets him judge
   tone before spending interview time. **Anyone who ignores the application instructions is
   dropped** — he reads that as the best effort the person will ever show. He later moved to a
   "we're hiring" page in the CRM: the post links to it, applicants fill a survey, and each
   lands in a **recruiting pipeline** (applied → round 1 → round 2 → top picks → hired). The
   pipeline is kept, so a failed hire is replaced by re-contacting earlier candidates. The page
   states the commission, the offer's price, and that leads are inbound — the founder is
   selling the job too.
3. **Interview in two rounds and score** (S-13 · "Interview SOP: … Setter/Closer"; S-14 ·
   lesson 274 "Interview SOP Hiring Appointment Setter/Closer"):
   - round 1 (founder alone in lesson 272; two interviewers in the SOP): fit with company and
     role (full-time? side businesses?), experience and low points, sales history (product,
     price point, volume, close rate, income, process, lead sources), goals;
   - a DISC / personality test between the rounds;
   - round 2, founder plus the existing sales team, who must sign off: DISC review, values and
     fit questions, their questions;
   - scorecard: 8 criteria × 1–5 per interviewer, two interviewers so one person's bias does
     not decide — drive, skill against the role's KPIs, coachability, honesty, clear direction,
     natural rapport, a believable reason for leaving, income goals that match the role (low
     goals count against). No pass mark stated.
   - red flags: cannot say why this company; no past failures; blames former bosses;
     unexplained job changes; neither market nor sales experience; ego; money as the only motive.
4. **Pay on results** (S-14 · lesson 270 "Determining Compensation for Setters and Closers
   (OTE)"; lesson 272). The comp plan exists to reward the one behaviour wanted — booking and
   closing front-end sales — not admin or delivery. Work it backwards from **on-target
   earnings (OTE)**: what a rep earns at the target close rate on the lead flow you supply. If
   OTE is too low, raise the percentage, raise lead flow, or add a small base.
   | Role | His pay | Industry OTE he quotes |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Setter | 5% of cash collected on closed calls they booked + $1K monthly bonus to the top setter | $3K–12K/month |
   | Closer | 10% of cash collected + $1K monthly bonus to the top closer | $8K–25K/month |
   | Hybrid (sets and closes own calls) | 15–20% (lesson 270) / ~15% (lesson 273) `[tag: conflict]`; optional base $1K–1.5K/month | — |
   All `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`. He pays **no base** because his lead flow is
   enough; he has seen others pay ~$1,500/month, and suggests a small base plus lower
   commission when volume is thin. He dislikes pay-per-set. Paid as contractors through a
   payroll service, logged as contractor expense.
5. **Define the roles** (S-14 · lesson 271 "Understanding the Roles of Setters and Closers in
   Sales & KPIs"):
   - setter: attends the morning meeting; calls and texts every inbound lead; qualifies and
     books calls for a closer; leaves CRM notes on the triage call; hands the lead to the
     closer (a three-way text introduction, or via the pipeline); chases no-shows; fills the
     tracking sheet and end-of-day report daily. Works from a setter pipeline.
   - closer: attends the morning meeting; runs 45–60 minute Zoom sales calls on the shared
     framework; works the pipeline in gaps between calls (no idle time); takes payment, sends
     the agreement and hands the client to onboarding; follows up deals and no-shows; fills the
     tracking sheet and end-of-day report. Works from a separate consultant pipeline.
6. **See their day** (S-14 · lesson 275 "Getting Access To Closers & Setters Google Cal";
   lesson 276 "Our Color Coding System For Google Cal Calls") — each rep gets a company email
   and shares their calendar with the manager with edit rights; the manager reads every rep's
   day side by side. Each call is coloured by outcome — follow-up, closed, rescheduled, no-show,
   showed-not-closed, confirmed, open, deposit, cancelled. A call not confirmed the day before
   is marked **open**: the slot is set to free so it can be double-booked rather than cancelled.
7. **Run the day** (S-13 templates; S-14 · lessons 277–280):
   - **Start-of-day meeting**, weekdays, **~15 minutes** (he holds it at 9am Eastern; move it
     for other time zones), agenda attached to the calendar invite: wins → each person shares
     their tracking sheet and pipeline on screen (an empty sheet means "you are leaving money on
     the table", and a rep who cannot work the leads given does not get more) → today's book by
     role (closers: calls booked, confirmed, closes they will stake their name on and the cash on
     them; setters: commit to a number of bookings for the open slots on closers' calendars;
     account managers: upsells, renewals and payment-plan instalments due) → gap to the monthly
     company goal and the plan to close it → week's pipeline and who is on the fence → blockers →
     call-review training Tue–Thu, alternating a setter's triage call and a closer's sales call →
     close.
   - **End-of-day report**, every rep, every day, as a form piped automatically into one chat
     channel (S-14 · lesson 278 "EOD Reporting For Sales Team"). Setter: inputs, calls offered
     and booked, set calls that closed, how the day went, where they need help, tracking sheet
     done?, then self-ratings 1–10 on energy, clarity, sense of urgency, inspiration, serving
     people, keeping promises to self, working at potential, and whether they will hit
     projections. Closer: revenue sold and collected, live calls, offers, enrolments, deposits,
     each call with its outcome or objection, plus the same self-ratings. He wants reps to judge
     themselves rather than be judged. A small team can drop the end-of-day meeting and keep
     only this form.
   - **End-of-day meeting, 15–30 min** (lesson 279): setters first (bookings, inputs, what they
     would do better, did they hit the morning commitment and if not how they close the gap) →
     closers walk through each call so setters learn which bookings were good → back-end sales,
     wins, hiring progress → tomorrow's calendar (confirmed? unconfirmed slots freed;
     setters asked to fill gaps tonight) → CRM hygiene (notes, follow-up tasks, pipeline
     stages) → the weekly goal → close.
   - **1:1s** every 1–2 weeks, 15–30 minutes (lesson 280): the team member submits priorities
     and asks ahead; the manager picks one of three question sets (personal life and
     productivity; engagement and what holds them back; alignment, culture and stop/keep
     feedback), ending with a 1–10 temperature check. **Underperformance may be a wrong-role
     problem, not a wrong-person problem** — he has moved people between sales and delivery and
     seen both improve.
   - Every team meeting and every sales call is recorded into its own channel; he reviews one or
     two sales calls every couple of days (S-14 · lesson 257 "Team Communication & What To Use
     (Slack)").
8. **Track, diagnose and forecast** (S-13 · sales dashboard; S-14 · lesson 281 "Tracking Sheet
   For Setters & Closers") — each rep logs daily: time on outreach, follow-up and new outreach,
   triage calls offered and booked, sales calls offered and booked, closes and cash; closers log
   new and follow-up meetings, connected calls, offers, follow-ups set, enrolments, first-call vs
   follow-up closes, deposits, down-sells, revenue sold and collected, lead source, upsells,
   referrals, and an end-of-day list of who closed for how much. The sheet shows each rep the gap
   to their own income goal. **Read the sheet as a story**: many hours but few messages → a 1:1;
   many first-call closes but few follow-up closes → the first call is not giving enough reason to
   come back; a low offer rate → unqualified bookings.
   Forecast: **closes needed = target ÷ average sale; connected calls = closes ÷ close rate;
   scheduled calls = connected ÷ show rate.**

## Decision points
- Constraint is delivery, not calls → do not hire sales; hire for delivery (ED-21).
- A rep below minimum for the week → training via call review (stated); a mismatch between
  hours and output → a 1:1; persistent underperformance → consider a different role (S-14 ·
  lesson 280). A firing rule is still not stated (H-16).
- A rep who hits "open to goal" early may stop for the day — a culture choice he leaves to the
  owner (lesson 271).

## Outputs
A staffed, measured sales team, a recruiting pipeline held in reserve, and a weekly forecast.

## Open edges
- **Employment classification.** He pays reps as commission-only 1099 contractors, yet sets
  required daily meetings at a fixed hour, daily quotas, company email and calendars, mandatory
  forms and personal-life 1:1 questions. Those controls are the features regulators weigh when
  deciding a worker is really an employee; commission-only pay can also fail minimum-wage and
  final-pay rules in some states and countries. Check before copying (H-16; proposed H-17).
- **Personal-data questions.** The 1:1 set asks about self-care and nights out, and the daily
  form asks reps to rate their own energy and state of mind; keep these voluntary and out of pay
  or firing decisions.
- **Personality tests in hiring** (DISC) are restricted or need justification in some places;
  use them as a talking point, not a filter.
- The setter "closed ÷ booked" metric and the hybrid commission rate are unclear in his own
  material.
- Hiring order across sales and delivery is now stated — see ED-21; H-9 narrows.
