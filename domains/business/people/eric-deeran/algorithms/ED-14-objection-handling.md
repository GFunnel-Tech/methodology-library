# ED-14 — Objection handling: clarify, discuss, diffuse

`source: stated` (S-14, section "Overcoming Objections Mastery", 37 video lessons, cited as
lesson 97–133) unless marked. No written document covers this section (it was H-11's main
gap). The call it sits inside is ED-07; commitment plays it hands off to are ED-15.

## Purpose
Treat an objection as a concern, find the real one behind the stated one, and resolve it in
conversation — without arguing, justifying or pushing — so the prospect can decide.

## When to use
Any time a prospect resists: on a triage call, mid-discovery, after price, or by text
between calls.

## Inputs
The pains, goal, gap, timeframe and time budget the prospect stated in discovery (ED-07);
a calendar; your package sheet.

## His premises (lessons 98, 102, 105, 107, 132)
1. **An objection is a concern, not an attack.** Do not take offence; do not go back into
   selling mode and justify.
2. **Most objections are caused earlier.** A stall after price means the gap was too small
   in discovery, trust was not built, or something in the call raised resistance — listen
   back to your recording first. "The sale is made in discovery."
3. **Most stalls are smokescreens** — "I'll get back to you", "I need to think", "send me
   info", "I need to ask my CPA" usually hide one real concern `[tag: assumption] stated by
   S-14 · lessons 105, 113, 128 as "99% of the time"`.
4. **Listen, validate, then ask** questions that lead the prospect to the conclusion; never
   sweep a concern under the rug — it comes back.
5. **Never handle an objection by text, DM or email** (lesson 99) — tone does not carry.
   Agree, and move the conversation to a call ("let's hop on anyway and at least make a
   plan for the future").

## Steps — the core formula (lesson 98)
1. **Clarify** — "how do you mean by [their word] specifically?" Never assume what "too
   expensive", "not the right time" or "I don't want to commit" means; each has several
   readings and each needs a different answer.
2. **Discuss** — hear the real reason; reflect it back; tie it to the goal and pains they
   gave you.
3. **Diffuse** — remove the concern (reset an expectation, adjust scope, pick a payment
   path, book a call with the missing person). He contrasts "diffuse" with "overcome".

He then gives five recurring moves (all `source: stated`; the grouping is ours):

- **M1 · Agree first ("that's not a problem")** — lowers pressure before any question
  (lessons 105, 111, 116, 119).
- **M2 · "Money aside, do you feel this would work for you?" → "why do you feel it would?"**
  — separates the money question from the fit question and makes the prospect argue for the
  fit (lessons 109, 111, 113).
- **M3 · "What's riskier?"** — risk of acting (money, time) set against risk of staying
  where they are, in their own goal's words (lessons 103, 104, 109, 113, 115, 124, 132).
- **M4 · "Maybe nothing / I'm not sure we can help yet"** — to competitor and "why you"
  questions, lean out and go back into discovery instead of listing strengths (lessons 117,
  119, 120, 121, 123).
- **M5 · Calendar commitment + "what were you hoping to go over?"** — for every stall, book a
  specific time in the next couple of days, then, as you are wrapping up, ask what they want
  to cover; the real concern usually comes out then (lessons 102, 105, 131; ED-15).

## By type (lesson 100: four types — time, money, competitor, trust/uncertainty)

### Time
| Objection | His move |
| --- | --- |
| "I'm busy, call me later" (lessons 101, 106, triage) | Don't offer to call at their convenience; offer a set time "so we don't chase each other"; or give your number and ask their timeframe. Framing yourself as busy keeps status. |
| "I need to think it over" / "I never make rash decisions" (lessons 102, 131) | Agree, book a time within days, then ask what they want to go over. Never "what do you need to think about? I thought you liked it" — a trap they see through. |
| "Not the right time" (lesson 103) | Clarify; ask how much time they think it needs; reset to the real commitment; then "how will you reach [goal] without it? whose choice is it? what's riskier?" |
| "I don't have the time" (lesson 104) | Clarify; they often think it needs far more hours than it does — reset; if still no, tie the minimal time to their goal; then M3. |
| "I'll get back to you" (lesson 105) | M1 + timeframe "in the next couple of days" + calendar commitment + M5. A follow-up weeks out is usually a dead deal. |

### Money
| Objection | His move |
| --- | --- |
| "Too expensive" (lessons 108, 115) | Clarify first: no value seen, a cheaper competitor, no funds, or a quote just above their number. If the last, offer the lower price with deliverables removed. Ask how they arrived at their figure, and whether it is enough to solve the problem. |
| "Better price?" (lesson 110) | Ask what's behind it; ask to see the competing quote in detail (it may not exist or may be a different service); then results vs. price; offer to match the price with matching scope. |
| "We don't have the money" / "I need to check my finances" (lessons 111, 114) | M1, M2; then "how could you find the funds to reach [goal]?", "what other avenues?"; finally suggest what other clients use (card, financing, another budget line). "Check my finances" is often only how to move the money. |
| "I don't want to use credit" (lesson 109) | M2; ask why; distinguish debt for a liability from investment in something that makes money; M3; if still no, a payment plan. **See open edges.** |
| "No budget left this year" (lesson 113, B2B) | The money exists but is allocated elsewhere; M2; "how could you resolve that?"; a consequence question; M3; then what other clients do (move budget between departments). |
| "You're more expensive than our vendor" (lesson 112) | Clarify; agree; "do you want the results you have now — which you said felt [their words] — or to solve that?" |

### Competitor
| Objection | His move |
| --- | --- |
| "We already have a vendor" / "happy with current" (lessons 117, 121) | M4: "you might be better off staying with them — I'd need to understand A, B, C first"; back to discovery. |
| "Talking to other companies" / "want to speak to others first" / "compare prices" (lessons 116, 119, 122) | Ask what they hope the others will say; then "if they all met your criteria, price included, how would you decide?" — they name the deciding factor. Offer to recommend someone better suited if you can't help. |
| "What's the difference between you and others?" (lessons 120, 123) | "Maybe nothing" + back to discovery; never "we're the best", which every competitor says. |
| "Just price shopping" (lesson 118) | Clarify; is price most important or solving the problem; a cheaper product may reflect a cheaper brand. |
| "I can do it with my phone / my cousin" / "I'll do it myself" (lessons 107, 132) | Agree it's possible; ask what result they want (amateur vs. professional); or ask technical how-will-you questions until the gap is felt; the cost of figuring it out; M3. Shifts "cost-based" to "results-based" thinking. |
| "Give me your pitch" (lesson 119) | Decline the pitch; ask a discovery question instead. |

### Trust and uncertainty
| Objection | His move |
| --- | --- |
| "Send me a quote / a proposal / some information" (lessons 125, 126, 130) | Never send instead of a call. Agree, ask what they hope to see, go into discovery; at the end, "if what I send meets everything, within budget — what's the next step?" Anything but "we'd start" means they're not serious. |
| "I don't want to commit to anything" (lesson 124) | Clarify; ask permission to disagree ("how could I tell you that you might be making the wrong decision without you getting upset?"); restate their pains; M3. |
| "We tried this before, it didn't work" (lesson 127) | Ask what didn't work; were others getting results with that provider; why not them — the answer names what they need ("so support matters to you") without you promising. |
| "Can I speak to your clients?" (lesson 128) | Agree; ask what they'd ask them; "if they echo everything, what's the next step?"; then connect them and book a follow-up. |
| "I need to talk to my CPA / partner / family" (lesson 129) | Best: have them on the call in the first place. Otherwise, ask how that person would feel about them reaching their goal; whether that person runs the business; if real permission is needed, book a call with that person present. |
| "Any guarantees?" (lesson 133) | Ask what's behind it; often a past provider walked away mid-job and the need is to be heard; answer the real fear (e.g. the agreement obliges us to finish the work). **See open edges.** |

## Decision points
- Real concern uncovered → resolve on the call; if not, book the next call with the concern
  known in advance.
- "If X checks out, what's next?" answered with anything but "we'd start" → treat as not
  serious; stop spending time (lessons 125, 128, 130).
- Literally no money and no way to fund → let them go (ED-07 step 2.10).
- Partner/approver must decide → book a call with them present; do not pitch through them.
- Stuck between yes and no → deposit close (ED-15).

## Outputs
The real objection named; it resolved, or a dated follow-up booked with it written down.

## Open edges
- **Pushing credit or financing** (lessons 109, 111, 113, 114): suggesting a card or funding
  to someone who says they cannot afford it or do not want debt, and "pay it off with the
  profits", implies a return nobody has measured. High risk with small businesses; in some
  places consumer-credit and income-claim rules apply. Our version: never suggest borrowing;
  offer a smaller scope or a later start.
- **"Maybe you should give up on the goal"** framing (lesson 104) and "whose choice is it?"
  (lesson 103) shame the prospect; teach the question, not the jab.
- **Unbacked comparative claims** (lessons 110, 115, 118): "cheap videos usually have to be
  redone", "a cheaper video reflects a cheaper brand", "what our clients tell us" — stated
  as fact with no evidence. Ours: only claims we can show.
- **"We guarantee our work"** (lesson 133) is a legal statement; it must match the agreement
  word for word, or not be said.
- **Scripted busyness** (lessons 101, 105, 106, 131): "I get pretty packed out", "I may not
  be randomly available" — fine if true, a lie if not. Ours: say only true things about your
  calendar.
- **"Act like you're leaving, then ask"** (lessons 105, 131) is a guard-lowering tactic; the
  question itself ("what do you want to cover next time?") is fine asked straight.
- **"I can recommend someone else"** (lesson 119) must be a real offer — keep a referral list.
- **M4 is only honest if you are actually unsure** whether you can help; said after you've
  decided, it is theatre.
- No method here for **letting go**: almost every path ends in another booked call. The
  canon's "escalate honestly" has no counterpart beyond the "not serious" test.

## Canon map
`source: derived`. **I.A.C.E.**: his *clarify* = **Clarify**; his "that's not a problem" +
validate = **Acknowledge**; *diffuse* = **Eliminate**; **Isolate** = his "money aside, would
this work?" and "if X checks out, what's next?" (both isolate one concern from the rest).
Missing: **escalate honestly** — when the concern is real and not solvable, the canon says
so and stops; he books another call. Most of his "it's a discovery problem" diagnoses point
back to the seven-step script: a weak **Pain Excavation** or **Vision Pull** shows up later
as a stall, which is why ED-07's framework questions (urgency, do-it-yourself, time) are
objection prevention. The four types (time, money, competitor, trust) are a usable intake
taxonomy for our own objection library. ACE stage: **C** (Creation — the seven-step script).
