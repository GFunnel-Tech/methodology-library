# ED-23 — LinkedIn automation outreach (profile → owner list → invite + two messages → conversation → call)

`source: stated` (S-14, section "Using LinkedIn Automation To Bring In Leads", lessons 58–69;
lesson 50 from "Prospecting") unless marked. One lesson (63) is taught by a member of his team
rather than by him; it is marked where used. The tool is **Waalaxy**, a LinkedIn automation
browser extension (the lesson titles spell it "Walaaxy").

## Purpose
Start one-to-one conversations with business owners at volume without sending every
connection request and opening message by hand, then turn replies into triage calls.

## When to use
B2B offers to owners, professionals and corporate or mid-market buyers; markets with a small
total addressable market where role-based targeting matters; as a low-budget bolt-on to paid
inbound (ED-02). He says it tolerates an offer and messaging that are not yet dialled in,
because each exchange is a conversation, not an ad (S-14 · lesson 59 "Overview").

## Inputs
A finished niche and offer statement (ED-05), the "problems you solve" list, a LinkedIn
profile, the automation extension, a booking calendar (ED-07).

## Steps (his order)
1. **Set expectations** (S-14 · lesson 58 "What To Expect: LinkedIn Automation"): launch early
   and let a campaign run two to two and a half weeks before judging it; follow the set-up
   exactly rather than improvising.
2. **Make the profile look like a working person** (S-14 · lesson 61 "LinkedIn Automated
   Prospecting - Intro & Setting Up Profile"): clean headshot, banner, a headline that is your
   offer statement ("I help [market] use [method] to [outcome]"), a link to your site, filled
   experience (remove jobs that do not help the pitch), skills, interests, contact info, and
   recent activity — he asks for at least about nine posts, so a visitor does not take you for
   an empty account. Content types come from ED-24.
3. **Message existing connections first** (S-14 · lesson 64 "Messaging Existing Connections: Low
   Hanging Fruit"): every current connection is a lead; open simply, chat, offer a call to
   learn about their role. A non-decision-maker can introduce you to the decision-maker by a
   three-way email. The point is reps: early sales calls teach more than instruction.
4. **Build a list** (S-14 · lesson 66 "How Create Your First List And Import Into Walaaxy"):
   search the niche keyword, filter to people, a location, and the title "owner" (or the role
   that buys, e.g. a marketing director); save the result as a named list (niche + location)
   and import it into the tool. Lesson 50 adds a manual variant: the same owner search, then
   find each person on Facebook to message there (ED-25).
5. **Set up the campaign** (S-14 · lesson 67 "How To Set Up Your First Walaaxy LinkedIn
   Campaign"): campaign type "invitation + two messages"; **no note** on the invitation;
   message one on acceptance, message two after about two days.
6. **Write the sequence** (S-14 · lesson 62 "Types of Questions and Messages for the Campaign"):
   - message one exists only to get a reply — short, no pitch, no block of text: thanks for
     connecting, you like to connect with people in their space;
   - message two, two days later: a light check-in plus one question about how long they have
     been in that space;
   - later follow-ups, about three days apart, short and not needy, just to sit at the top of
     the inbox; you may add a link to your VSL or lead magnet.
   On acceptance he also likes or comments on a couple of their posts so it feels personal
   (S-14 · lesson 60 "How LinkedIn Automation Works").
7. **Take over by hand when they reply** (lessons 60, 62): you or an appointment setter. Ask
   probing questions drawn from the problems-you-solve list — a human conversation, close to
   the triage script (ED-07). After three or four exchanges, offer a short 5–10 minute call
   to learn more, with "at the least I'll send you tips or a game plan" as the low-pressure
   reason. Then triage → sales call → close (ED-07).
8. **Alternative: the networking frame** (S-14 · lesson 63 "Walaaxy Networking Frame", taught
   by a team member): message one asks them to *confirm they still work in the role* their
   profile shows (profiles go stale); message two asks for their help with a few questions
   about how people in their role operate. In the conversation, play the learner: state your
   assumptions about how their business gets clients so they correct you and name the real
   problem in their own words. After three to five questions, propose a networking call; book
   it on a **separate calendar** without the sales-call reminder sequence, because they did
   not ask to be sold. Near the end they usually ask what you do; give a one-line description
   (no packages), ask whether that kind of help would benefit them, and offer a second call.
9. **Monitor** (S-14 · lesson 68 "How To Monitor Analytics On Your Messaging Campaigns"):
   track invitations sent, acceptance rate and first-message response rate; let campaigns run
   for several weeks in the background.
10. **Keep the queue full** (S-14 · lesson 69 "How To Continue To Add Prospects To Your Queue"):
   every week to week and a half, check the queue is not empty; if it is, re-run the search,
   re-import (the tool skips duplicates) into the same running campaign.

## Decision points
- Acceptance or response below target after the run-in period → change the profile, the list
  or the opener, one at a time (`source: derived`; he gives the targets, not the order).
- They reply → stop the automation and go manual.
- Prospect is not the decision-maker → ask for an introduction, not a pitch.
- Sales-frame or networking-frame: networking reads warmer but the prospect is "not bought
  into anything", so the second call has to do the selling (lesson 63).

## Outputs
A running background campaign, a stream of conversations, booked triage or networking calls.

## Open edges
- **Account-ban risk.** LinkedIn's User Agreement prohibits third-party software, extensions or
  bots that automate activity or scrape profiles; automation tools are a common reason
  accounts are restricted or closed. Teaching this as-is puts a learner's main professional
  account at risk. Our version: manual sending within LinkedIn's limits, or a tool used
  knowing the risk, never on an account the learner cannot afford to lose.
- **Data protection.** Importing profiles into a third-party tool processes personal data;
  for EU/UK prospects GDPR applies (a stated lawful basis such as legitimate interest, a
  privacy notice on request, honouring objections). Not addressed in S-14.
- **Honesty of automated messages.** A scripted "just had a minute between meetings" sent by
  software states something untrue; the networking frame asks for "help with questions"
  while the purpose is a sale. Our version keeps the light tone but states who you are and why
  you are writing before a call is booked.
- The two daily/monthly volume figures disagree, and LinkedIn's current weekly invitation cap
  is not stated anywhere in S-14 (held open).
- No attribution of booked calls or closed deals back to a campaign or list.

## Canon map
`source: derived`. **ACE Acquisition:** a list per niche and location is one *attention
channel*; the tool's acceptance and response rates are the first half of "track ROI per
channel", but S-14 stops before cost per booked call or per client, so reallocation between
LinkedIn, Facebook groups and ads cannot yet be done on evidence. The automated first two
messages are "automate first response" in reverse (automated first *contact*), with the
human taking over on reply. **Brand and Identity:** the profile step is consistency across
touchpoints — the headline repeats the offer statement (ED-05) and the feed shows proof — and
the networking frame's "state assumptions to be corrected" is how the current state is
measured before the promised state is offered.
