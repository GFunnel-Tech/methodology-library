# ED-07 — Booking form, triage call, show rate and the full closing call

`source: stated` unless marked. Two sources: the written documents (S-13, section "Discovery
Call Workflow + Sales Meeting & Closing Deals" and "Setting Up Your Call Calendar") and the
section's 27 video lessons (S-14). Items first found in the videos are marked **[S-14]**.
Objection handling is its own file (ED-14); commitment and rescue plays (deposit close,
trial terms, payment splits, ghost text, same-call upsell) are ED-15.

## Purpose
Qualify before the long call, get the prospect to show up prepared, then run one call that
does the selling in discovery — widen the gap between where they are and where they want to
be — so the price at the end is a formality rather than a fight.

## When to use
Every inbound or outbound lead that wants to talk; every follow-up meeting with the same
prospect (the opening changes, the shape does not).

## Inputs
A booking page in the CRM, a calendar, the "problems you solve" list turned into questions,
your 4–5 pillars, a package sheet with three term lengths, a call recorder, a written
agreement reviewed by a lawyer.

## Steps (his order)

### A. Before the call
1. **Booking-form questions** (S-13 · "Qualifying Questions…"): name and phone; confirm they
   are the owner and will bring every decision-maker; type of business and what sets it
   apart; top priorities this year; biggest current challenges; average monthly revenue;
   past spend on video and why; anything else. No automatic cut-off on this form.
2. **Triage call** (S-13 · "How To Book Triage Calls"; S-14 · lessons 76/85 "How To Book
   Triage Calls" / "How To Triage & Book Calls", lesson 86 "Triage Call Example"), a short
   call to someone who opted in but did not book:
   1. what made them reach out;
   2. who they are — established or just starting; **never had a client or customer →
      disqualify**;
   3. their biggest goal, in numbers;
   4. where they are now against it (clients, typical price, monthly revenue);
   5. what they have tried (nothing → find out why and take doing it alone off the table;
      something → the biggest challenge);
   6. timing — if not now, when and why then;
   7. can they give about 1–3 hours a week `[tag: assumption] stated by S-13 and S-14 · lesson 76`;
   8. offer the full video call as a question ("would that be helpful?"), not a demand
      **[S-14 · lesson 95]**; confirm ~45 minutes, at a computer, somewhere quiet;
   9. partners or co-decision-makers must attend;
   10. money: in the documents, state the package range if money has not come up; in the
       videos, only as a last check for someone who looks financially weak ("would anything
       get in the way financially if this felt like a good fit?") `[tag: conflict]` S-13 vs
       S-14 · lesson 76 on when to raise money.
   Tone: natural pauses, disarming tone, neutral words; do not over-qualify good leads.
   In the worked example the triage caller books the prospect with a named second person
   ("our founder") rather than themselves **[S-14 · lesson 86]**.
3. **Protect the show rate** (S-13 · "How To Fix No Show Rates"; S-14 · lesson 95, same
   title), checked in this order: book only 48–72 hours ahead; keep realistic working-hours
   availability; cancel bad fits; send no more than the VSL and a thank-you video; automate
   reminders; call self-booked leads first to triage; check that the setter asked rather than
   pushed the booking; send a short personal screen-recorded video; at the call time, call
   twice in a row; move no-shows to a CRM stage that starts a five-day no-show sequence;
   double-book only as a last resort.
4. **Practise before you sell** **[S-14 · lesson 70 "What To Expect: Sales Calls"]**:
   customise the script, then role-play it alone, with family, with the coach; record and
   post calls for review. Follow the process exactly first; change it only once it works.
5. **Record every sales and client call; always use a written agreement** **[S-14 ·
   lessons 71, 72]**. Recording protects both sides on what was promised; he names a free
   recorder that also reports talk time, used to check the prospect talks most. He gives
   his agreement template as "recreational", to be reviewed by your own lawyer.

### B. The call — five parts (S-14 · lesson 73 "Overview Of The Sales Process", lesson 79 "Workflow Map Out")
Intro/rapport → discovery → diagnosis → collections → objections. The doctor analogy runs
through it: ask where it hurts, diagnose, prescribe. His central claim: **the sale is made in
discovery, not in the pitch** — "the quality of the questions you ask" decides the close.

6. **Intro and rapport, short** — no weather talk. He gives 15–30 seconds (lesson 79) and
   "no more than 5% of the call" (lesson 73) `[tag: conflict]` (5% of a 45-minute call is
   about 2 minutes). Then ask what made them take the call.
7. **Pre-frame the call** — "look at what you have going on, maybe give some advice, and if
   it might be a fit, go over options to work with us — fair?" (lesson 79). The pre-frame
   must say an offer may come at the end; a prospect who thinks the call is a free
   collaboration is the hardest to close (lesson 94, a student call review — title withheld because it names the student). On a follow-up
   call: "has anything changed since we last talked?", then restate their problem.
8. **Decision-makers** — ask whether they run the business alone; if a partner (or a
   franchise head office, lesson 91, a student call review) has a say, get that person on
   a call before pricing rather than pitching through the absent person.
9. **Checklist (discovery)** — two kinds of question:
   - *niche questions*, each made from a problem your service solves ("how are you currently
     showcasing your credibility through video?") — the "problems you solve" exercise
     (S-13; S-14 · lesson 77);
   - *chunk-down questions* when a pain appears: how long, what has it cost, how is it
     affecting the business — dig, do not move on (lessons 79, 87 "Discovery Example").
   Let them say the obvious pain in their own words; asking an "obvious" question is the
   point, because people act on what they say themselves (lesson 87).
10. **Framework questions, always asked, at the end of the checklist** (lesson 79):
    - goal, then "is there any significance behind that number?";
    - current position, then **state the gap** back ("so about a $500k gap to close — right?");
    - how long they have been trying;
    - **urgency**: "why change anything now? why not keep doing what you're doing?" — let
      them argue for change;
    - **do-it-yourself**: "why not do this yourself / hire in-house?" — let them say why not;
    - **time**: how much time they can give; then how soon they want it in place, and if not
      soon, how they chose that date.
    These are asked to remove the late objections (no time, I'll do it myself, not now)
    before price, "because after the price drops, every concern becomes an objection".
11. **Quantify the outcome** — turn the goal into money where you can (extra brand deals,
    extra clients × average client value); a prospect who "can't see the return" is one you
    did not show the return to (lesson 94).
12. **Optional two-call close** — stop after the checklist, say you will build a custom
    strategy, and book the next call in 2–3 days; the second call opens at diagnosis
    (lesson 79). He mostly closes on one call (lesson 88) — the two-call version is an option,
    not his default.
13. **Diagnosis** — "I feel like I have everything I need; what would you like to do from
    here — some advice, next steps?" If they ask the price now, defer: "we're about to get
    into that — can I walk you through some action steps to get you to [their goal]?"
14. **Pitch the pillars against their pains** (S-13 · "4-5 Pillar Exercise"; S-14 · lessons
    78, 79, 91). One sentence pattern, repeated for each of 2–4 pains, the named step:
    > "Remember how you told me [pain]? Because of that, we're going to [pillar] so that you
    > can [outcome]."
    Then "does that make sense?". A pillar is a real stage of the work, never "onboarding
    call". Sell "the vacation, not the plane ride" — outcomes, not a deliverables list
    (lesson 94).
15. **Two sales in every call** **[S-14 · lesson 82]** — first sell the *method* (why this
    approach is the fastest way from A to B), then *you* as the one to deliver it. Most
    sellers skip the first.
16. **Temperature check before price** (lessons 79, 91, 94): "do you feel this could
    actually work for you?" then "why do you feel it would?" — make them say it. Price is
    shown only when two things are true: **they believe they need it, and now is the right
    time**. "Price without context has no value." A one-to-ten scale is optional; he finds it
    salesy (lesson 79), the closer in lesson 90 used it.
17. **Answer questions before price, briefly** — make a game of the shortest true answer;
    before answering, find out what is behind the question ("can I ask what you mean
    specifically?") — his example: "do you film at my office?" hid "I'm embarrassed by my
    office" (lesson 79).
18. **Collections** (lessons 79, 80, 91, 94): share the package sheet; name three options
    (e.g. 12, 6, 3 months), optionally "most clients do [middle]", then ask "which one are you
    leaning towards?" and **stay silent** however long it takes. Do not walk through each
    package after showing price.
19. **On yes, say one thing** — "how do you want to take care of that?" Take payment, then
    send the agreement to be "authorized" (S-14 · lesson 84, his wording replaces "sign the
    contract"; ED-15 open edges). No congratulations speech — talking after the yes loses
    deals (lessons 79, 88).
20. **Objections** → ED-14; **can't decide** → deposit close (ED-15).
21. **Close and track** (S-13 · sales dashboard): outcomes recorded as first-call close,
    follow-up close, deposit, or down-sell; revenue sold vs. collected; referrals asked.

### C. Conduct throughout
- **Neutral language, detached from outcome** **[S-14 · lesson 74]**: "might", "possibly",
  "I'm not sure we can help yet". Whoever leans out draws the other in.
- **80/20 talk time** **[S-14 · lesson 80]**: the prospect talks 80%; most of your 20% is the
  pillar pitch. In lesson 73 the pitch is "about 10% of the meeting" — consistent.
- **Never ask the budget before discovery** **[S-14 · lesson 75]**: a budget reflects the
  problem as the prospect sees it before discovery has shown its size.
- **Authority on camera** **[S-14 · lesson 94]**: slower, centred speech; no filler words;
  still body; dress a little smarter. The prospect reads your conviction, especially at price.
- **Never send a proposal or quote instead of a call** (S-14 · lessons 125, 126; ED-14).
- **It is an outline, not a script** — order may flex as the conversation flows, as long as
  you reach the gap (lesson 87).

## Decision points
- No prior customers → disqualify. Partners or approvers not on the call → book a call with
  them before price.
- Prospect not bought in on need *and* timing → do not show price; go back to discovery.
- Money is a blocker → before the full call (S-13) or as a last triage check (S-14).
- Can't decide after objection handling → deposit close (ED-15) or a calendar commitment.

## Outputs
A booked, attended call; a recorded call; a stated gap; a pillar pitch tied to their pains;
a chosen package and payment, or a scheduled next step with the real concern named.

## Open edges
- **Recording calls**: he says always record; recording law differs by place (some require
  every party's consent). Our version: announce recording at the start, every time.
- **"Recreational" agreement**: he tells students to have it reviewed; do not treat his
  template as fit to use.
- **Pre-framing** must say plainly that an offer may follow — his lesson 94 shows the cost of
  not doing so; we should make it a rule, not a tip.
- **Quantified-gap questions** can turn into invented numbers ("you're paying life $900k a
  year", lesson 88, credited by him to another speaker). Keep the arithmetic the prospect's
  own and honest.
- **Price examples** are one seller's packages in one niche (video); not benchmarks.
- H-5 is now closed in substance by S-14; what stays open is whether the current paid
  program (S-10) has changed the outline.
