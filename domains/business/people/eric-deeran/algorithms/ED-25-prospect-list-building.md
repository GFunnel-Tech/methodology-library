# ED-25 — Prospect list building (warm network → groups → owner search → cross-platform match)

`source: stated` (S-14, section "Prospecting", lessons 43–50; lesson 64 from the LinkedIn
section) unless marked. This is step 1 of the outbound loop in ED-06; messaging is ED-06,
automation is ED-23.

## Purpose
Produce a list of reachable decision-makers in the chosen niche so the daily conversation
quota (ED-06) never runs dry, starting from people who already know you.

## When to use
Before the first outbound day, and whenever the list or the automation queue runs low.

## Inputs
The niche from ED-05 (its "easy to target" test is what makes this step possible), access to
your own email, social and LinkedIn accounts, a CRM or tracking board.

## Steps (his order — warmest first)
1. **Email contacts** (S-14 · lesson 45 "List - Email Contacts"): export everyone you have
   emailed before from your mail account into the CRM; reach out to a fixed number a day.
2. **Your own social followings** (S-14 · lesson 46 "List - All Social Media Followings"):
   treat every friend and follower as a lead, however small the following; message each one,
   either asking whether they know someone in your niche who needs this help, or whether they
   are that person (the indirect ask of ED-06).
3. **Existing LinkedIn connections** (S-14 · lesson 64): the same, for every connection.
4. **Facebook groups where the niche gathers** (S-14 · lesson 47 "List - Facebook Group
   Prospecting"): join groups for the niche *and* groups for the niche's own customers (gym
   owners recruit in fitness groups, so they are members there too). Open the member list,
   expand it fully, search the page for "owner", and for each owner send a friend request
   first, then a short message that opens a conversation as a person, before the ED-06 flow.
5. **Paid-audience "challenge" groups** (S-14 · lesson 48 "List - Using Facebook "Challenge
   Groups" To Get Leads"): coaches run ads into free multi-day challenges whose group fills
   with your exact niche, already in pain and expecting to spend. He teaches joining these,
   attending the sessions so you can refer to them, finding members who share a mutual
   friend, opening on that connection, and offering a done-for-you free trial as a better
   offer than the coach's done-with-you programme. See open edges — this is the riskiest play
   here.
6. **LinkedIn groups** (S-14 · lesson 49 "List - LinkedIn Groups"): join niche groups; being in
   the same group lets you message members who are not connections; search the member list
   for "owner" and open conversations.
7. **LinkedIn owner search → Facebook match** (S-14 · lesson 50 "List - Hybrid - LinkedIn & FB
   Prospecting"): search LinkedIn for the niche keyword, filter to people, a country and title
   "owner"; take a name, search it on Facebook with the niche word added; the results show
   that person and similar owners; friend them, message them. Adding them as friends also puts
   your niche in your own audience for later posts (ED-24). A manager can be a route to the
   owner.
8. **Log and keep going**: every list goes into the CRM or tracking board; pick one platform to
   work first — the one with the most contacts (S-14 · lesson 43).

## Decision points
- A group you cannot join, or that asks qualifying questions → see open edges; do not
  misrepresent who you are.
- Not the decision-maker → ask to be introduced.
- Warm contacts exhausted → move to groups and searches; never skip the warm list.

## Outputs
A growing, de-duplicated list per niche and platform, each person with where they were found.

## Open edges
- **Pretexting.** He suggests joining owner-only groups by saying you are "looking to start" a
  business, and opening with a customer-style question ("do you offer discounts to new
  customers?") as if you were a buyer. Both misrepresent who you are; they can break group
  rules and platform terms and erode trust once the pitch comes. Our version: join groups you
  qualify for, and open as yourself.
- **Challenge-group poaching.** Recruiting another seller's paid audience inside their group,
  and pitching against their offer, usually breaks the group's rules and the platform's spam
  policies and invites reports and bans. The "five leads in a week" free-trial line is a
  results promise and needs the safeguards of H-15.
- **Platform limits.** Facebook and LinkedIn restrict accounts that send many friend requests or
  messages to strangers; the 50–100 a day figure risks that. Daily caps are not stated in S-14.
- **Email.** An exported contact list is not an opt-in list. Commercial email must meet
  CAN-SPAM (US: honest headers and subject, a physical address, a working opt-out honoured
  within 10 business days) and, for EU/UK recipients, GDPR/PECR (consent or a narrow existing-
  customer exception). Texts need prior consent under the TCPA (ED-09).
- **Copying member lists into a CRM** is processing personal data; under GDPR it needs a lawful
  basis and a way to honour objections.
- No measurement of which list source produces calls or clients.

## Canon map
`source: derived`. **ACE Acquisition:** each source (email list, own followers, a group, a
search) is an attention channel; step 8's "one platform first" and single board are the
*centralize* move; the missing piece is *tag at capture* — record the source on every
contact so calls and clients can be traced to it and effort *reallocated* from weak to strong
sources instead of by habit. **Brand and Identity:** friending the niche before posting
(step 7) builds the audience that the content's *frequency/audience match* depends on; the
warm-first order reflects that trust already exists on the warm end of the gradient.
