# ED-21 — Time audit and hiring sequence: find the $10 tasks, then hire in order

`source: stated` (S-14, sections "Time Management And Audit" and "Hiring/Managing/Leading
Your Team") unless marked.

## Purpose
Get the founder out of low-value work in the right order. A seven-day time audit shows where
the hours actually go; a fixed sequence of hires, tied to monthly revenue, removes delivery
first and sales last; a rule — hire only to remove a constraint, and try a system before a
person — keeps the team from outgrowing the margin.

## When to use
When the founder is working long hours and revenue is flat (his diagnosis: you are working on
the wrong things), and before every hire.

## Inputs
A week of the founder's working time, the P&L (ED-13), the current monthly revenue, the
pipeline numbers (ED-11).

## Steps
1. **Run a seven-day time audit** (S-14 · lesson 24 "Complete Our Time Audit Sheet"). Log what
   you are doing every 15–30 minutes, with a start/stop timer or entered by hand. Tag each task
   on four columns:
   - **revenue-generating?** — does it directly bring money in? Calling leads and taking sales
     calls are yes; editing client work, driving to a shoot, a delivery meeting and managing
     tasks are no (he allows that the gym can count, because it makes you better on calls);
   - **cost to hire out**, on a four-step $ to $$$$ scale, like restaurant price tags;
   - **do you enjoy it?** — yes / no / neutral, recorded but not decisive: high-value work you
     dislike still stays with you;
   - **total and daily average time**.
   He says trends show by day two to four, and that the act of logging changes behaviour.
2. **Filter to the non-revenue hours** and price them (S-14 · lesson 23 "Why Is A Time Audit
   Important"; lesson 24): compare what the hour costs to hire out with what the same hour earns
   on a sales call. His rule of thumb: you will not build a large business on tasks someone else
   would do for $10–20 an hour. Review the sheet on a coaching call and list what to delegate.
3. **Accept "80% done by someone else"** (lesson 23) — the objection "they won't do it as well as
   me" is answered by training them, not by keeping the task. He credits the line to a mentor.
4. **Before hiring, name the constraint** (S-14 · lesson 269 "When To Hire Closers"; lesson 268
   "When To Hire Setters"). Hire only to remove a current or near-future bottleneck; a hire with
   no bottleneck behind it is an oversized team and thinner margin. Match the hire to the
   bottleneck: drowning in delivery → a delivery hire, not a setter and more ad spend.
5. **Try a system before a person** (lesson 269) — "nine times out of ten" he first looks for an
   automation (copying data between tools, routine notifications), then an operations assistant
   or VA for what is left, and only then a specialist.
6. **Hire in this order**, by monthly revenue (S-14 · lesson 256 "30,000ft View: Hiring Cadence &
   Sequence"). Three buckets — marketing, sales, fulfilment — and at the start the founder holds
   every role in all three. All bands `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`; he calls them estimates,
   not thresholds.
   | Phase | Revenue/month | New hire | Founder still holds |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | 1 | $10–25K | editor (most time-consuming, earns nothing directly, cheap to find) | everything else |
   | 2 | $25–60K | videographer (shooting days go back into sales) | account management, all sales, all marketing |
   | 3 | $60–100K | account manager (client liaison between client and crew; runs the client's board and chat, routes footage and feedback) | client success director, sales, marketing, media buying, ad creative |
   | 4 | $100–150K | commission-only appointment setter | closing, sales management, marketing |
   | 5 | (no figure given) | closer (the hardest hire: last person before the money); optionally a media buyer and an in-house ad-creative person | client success director, sales manager, marketing director |
7. **Stay in sales as long as possible** (lesson 256; lesson 269). Delivery is important — it
   prevents refunds and earns renewals — but it does not bring the money in up front. Handing off
   sales too early loses what he calls the founder's "magic" on the call: revenue drops while costs
   rise. The founder's own calls also become the training library and the close-rate baseline for
   the first closer (ED-11). His analogy: at first you play every position on the team; revenue
   buys better players, position by position, the scoring positions last.

## Decision points
- Long hours, flat revenue → time audit first; the problem is allocation, not tactics (lesson 24).
- A task is low value and cheap to hire out → delegate now, whatever you enjoy.
- A task can be automated → automate before hiring.
- Delivery is the bottleneck → hire for delivery; calls are the bottleneck → setter, then closer.

## Outputs
A seven-day time log, a delegation list priced in $–$$$$, and the next hire named by the
constraint it removes.

## His stated examples (all `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`)
- Hand off 40 hours of editing at $25–30/hour (~$1,000/week), spend the 40 hours on one-hour
  sales calls, close even 10% at a $10K ticket → $40K/week. The multiplication is `[tag:
  forced]`; the inputs are not. **The example assumes 40 qualified calls a week are available**,
  which is the lead-flow problem his own setter and closer prerequisites warn about
  (ED-11 step 1) `[tag: conflict]` — the hours freed are only worth the calls the funnel can fill.
- A student spending ~122 hours a year on admin could have hired it out at ~$10/hour and taken the
  same number of sales calls; at 10% and a ~$30K ticket that is "a couple hundred thousand
  dollars". Same caveat. (The student is not named here.)
- One audited day showed about seven non-revenue hours.
- Falsifier for all: our own audit plus our own calls-available count.

## Open edges
- Phase 5 has no revenue figure, and marketing roles (media buyer, ad creative) float between
  phases 3 and 5.
- The sequence is written for video and content agencies; for a non-production service the
  "editor" slot is whatever delivery task takes the most founder hours (`source: derived`).
- The time audit counts hours, not energy or deep-work blocks; there is no planning step after
  delegation (a weekly calendar or time blocks) — the operating rhythm is the meeting cadence in
  ED-11.
- Recording personal activities (the gym) is optional; a team-wide audit would raise privacy
  questions not covered.

## Canon map
- **Time Management** — the audit is measure-before-reallocate: classify every hour by value and
  replacement cost, then move the founder's hours to the highest-value use. The canon's
  time-management work would add the calendar step he leaves out. `source: derived`
- **Hiring Algorithm** — steps 4–6 state the role's structural purpose (the constraint it
  removes) before the role is filled, which is the canon's first move; "system before person" is a
  cheaper test of that purpose. The four pillars, references, Immersion onboarding and 30/60/90
  reviews are not in this sequence (see ED-11, ED-12). `source: derived`
- **ACE Expansion** — hiring delivery first is what frees capacity to serve more clients;
  hiring sales last protects Conversion while Expansion is built. `source: derived`
- Theory of constraints, as in ED-01 and ED-22: one bottleneck at a time.
