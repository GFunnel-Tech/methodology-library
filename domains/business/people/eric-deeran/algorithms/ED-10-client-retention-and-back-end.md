# ED-10 — Client retention: fulfillment KPIs, satisfaction survey, back-end offers

`source: stated` (S-13, sections "Onboarding and Client Management/Client Journey" and
"New Client Checklist") unless marked.

## Purpose
Keep clients longer and sell them the next thing, by measuring delivery and asking for
feedback.

## When to use
From the first week of a new client, and at each renewal point.

## Inputs
The client list with start and end dates, what each paid, the CRM.

## Steps
1. **Intake the client's customer avatar** (S-13 · "Getting Your Client Demographics"): age,
   gender, income, location, interests and pages followed, pain points, dream outcome
   (political leaning optional). Used for ad targeting.
2. **Measure delivery** (S-13 · "Client Fulfillment KPIs"):
   - retention — average client lifespan (3, 6 or 12 months); if clients leave, find out
     whether it was results or return on investment;
   - churn % per month;
   - **time to first value** — days to the first result, financial or not (e.g. the first
     batch of content); he calls shortening it one of the strongest retention levers;
   - average client lifetime value; raise it by meeting expectations, then upsell,
     cross-sell or downsell;
   - offer review — what the first offer usually achieves, whether what was pitched was
     delivered, whether the client sees the long-term path.
3. **Survey clients** (S-13 · "Client Satisfaction Survey"), built in the CRM and read on the
   contact record. Order: identity → most and least helpful parts → experience 1–10 →
   likelihood to recommend 1–10 → favourite part → what would get faster results → open
   feedback, explicitly inviting criticism.
4. **Sell the back end** (S-13 · "What Are Back-End Offers?"), three kinds:
   **continuation** (renew the retainer), **next problem** (content done → site, ads,
   campaigns), **retention downsell** (a smaller, cheaper monthly offer for a satisfied
   client who no longer needs the full one).

## Decision points
- Client leaving → diagnose results vs. ROI before offering anything.
- Satisfied but needs less → downsell rather than lose them.

## His stated figures
- Example lifetime value $25k `[tag: assumption] stated by S-13; one example`.
- Churn example: 2 leaving against 10 new = 20%. `source: derived` note: that divides
  leavers by new clients, not by active clients; we compute churn as leavers ÷ clients at
  the start of the month `[tag: conflict]`.

## Outputs
A KPI sheet per client base, survey answers on each contact, a back-end offer per client.
