# ED-17 — Lead magnet choice, ad and VSL scripting by funnel stage, and on-camera production

`source: stated` (S-14, section "Creating Your VSL & Ads (For Your Agency)", lessons 165–202,
plus the creative lessons in "Setting Up Ads" and "Facebook Ads Course") unless marked.
Structures and reasons only, in our words; no script lines are copied. The nine member VSLs
and nine member ads (lessons 181–190, 194–202) are summarised as patterns; their businesses
are not named.

## Purpose
Decide what to offer in exchange for a lead, write the ad and the video sales letter (VSL) so
each speaks to the buyer's stage, and record them so they hold attention. This is the
creative layer that ED-16 runs and ED-08 measures.

## When to use
Before a campaign launches, and each time the decision tree in ED-16 says "change the
creative".

## Inputs
Your niche and the outcome you sell (ED-05), your 4–5 pillars (ED-07), client results and
testimonials, your guarantee, competitors' running ads, a camera or phone.

## Steps (his order)

1. **Choose the lead magnet by how ready the buyer is** (S-14 · lesson 166 "Different Types
   Of Lead Magnets & Buyer Personas").

   | Lead magnet | Who it attracts | Volume and cost | Nurture needed |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Free workshop or resource | the least qualified; many freebie-seekers | most leads, cheapest | long email/text sequences |
   | Case study | problem-aware people a little further along | middle | moderate |
   | VSL / pitch video | people who know the problem, have looked for a fix and want it now | fewest leads, dearest | least |

   He uses all three together. His picture of a market: about 3% ready to buy now, 17%
   gathering information, 20% aware of the problem but not yet looking, 60% not yet aware
   `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14; source of the split not given (H-ED17-1)`. Nurture
   length grows with distance from buying: short for information-gatherers, about 2–4
   months for the problem-aware, about 4–6 months for the unaware `[tag: assumption] stated
   by S-14`. His school's default is to go for the bottom of the funnel first, because it
   gives the quickest wins (lessons 173, 191).

2. **Build every ad from three parts** (S-14 · lessons 167 "3 Main Components Of An Ad", 170
   "The Value/Offer Explained", 171 "Call To Action Explained"): a **hook** that names who it
   is for (the first ~3 seconds), a **body** (the context and value — what you do, for whom,
   why to believe it) and a **call to action** that says exactly what to do next. Missing
   the call to action after earning attention is named as a common, avoidable error.

3. **Write and test hooks first** (S-14 · lesson 168 "The Hook Explained"; lesson 169
   "Examples Of Hook Variations"). Most viewers never get past the hook, so he tests the
   most hooks, fewer bodies and the fewest calls to action. Four hook types:
   - **label** — name the audience and tell them to listen;
   - **yes-question** — a question about a pain they will answer yes to;
   - **if–then** — "if you have this problem, it is because of that";
   - **ridiculous result** — lead with a striking client result.
   Make the opening stand out in a feed: visual contrast (bright clothing or background),
   movement in the shot, a presenter who resembles the audience (age, gender, background),
   a setting the audience recognises as theirs (a workshop for trades, a house for solar),
   and *show, don't tell* with b-roll of the pain. He reports that a woman presenter hired
   for one of his ads beat him `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14, single test`. Lesson 169
   is a reel of one client's dozens of hook variants on one body — the volume is the point.
   `source: stated`

4. **Choose the body from a parts list** (S-14 · lesson 172 "Ad Body Alternatives"): audience,
   hook, offer, guarantee, proof and testimonials, **epiphany bridge** (you or a client went
   through a struggle and came out the other side), track record (why you can claim what you
   claim), **context** (why this problem deserves attention), mechanism explanation (what you
   do, at a chosen level of detail), **old way / new way** (what most people do, why it
   fails, what it costs them, the new way and who is succeeding with it), call to action.

5. **Match the body to the funnel stage** (S-14 · lesson 191 "Variable Ad Scripting
   Explained"):
   - *Top of funnel* (unaware): hook with audience, a long **context** section that makes
     them notice the problem, a soft call to action ("click for more information"). Expect
     these to take most impressions and few conversions; their job is to teach the platform
     who responds.
   - *Middle of funnel* (open to solutions): hook, short context, old way / new way, a light
     mechanism, broad social proof, a medium call to action ("see how it works").
   - *Bottom of funnel* (ready): hook, short context, a specific mechanism, **the offer**,
     detailed social proof, track record, a strong call to action (fill in the form, see if
     you qualify, book a call).
   He says plainly that his example scripts for this lesson were written to teach, not run
   `source: stated`. The two ready-made scripts — offer-forward (bottom) and indirect free
   system (middle) — are in ED-08 step 1 (S-13, confirmed by S-14 · lesson 192 "2 Types Of
   Video Ad Scripts + Full Script"). In the indirect script the pitch is for the free thing,
   never for hiring you; the "why is this free" line is honest (we earn trust; take it and do
   it yourself if you like).

6. **Write the VSL in seven parts** (S-14 · lesson 179 "VSL (Video Sales Letter) Full Example +
   Script"; refines ED-08 step 2):
   1. *Opening* — who is speaking, in one line.
   2. *Direct call-out* — the niche and the outcome they want; this also pushes away
      everyone else.
   3. *Erase doubts* — feel, felt, found: you may feel X; I felt the same until Y; then I
      found Z.
   4. *Name your method* — give your system a new name and list its 4–5 pillars by name. His
      reason: a buyer who has been pitched "short-form content" a hundred times already has a
      view of it, often bad, and dismisses you before you start; a name they have never
      heard carries no prior verdict `source: stated`.
   5. *Reframe* — tell them what really matters (his: people buy from businesses they know,
      like and trust, not from the best product), the way a salesperson decides which
      feature of a car is "the most important" `source: stated`.
   6. *Reveal and tutorial* — each step in one or two sentences. One line is the method in
      his own words: "the what, not the how." The tutorial shows enough to prove you know
      the way and makes clear that doing it alone is a lot of work, so hiring you is the
      reasonable next step.
   7. *Offer, guarantee and call to action* — book a call to see if you are a fit.
   He says "seven pillars" but names six in the audio, with the method introduced between
   doubts and reframe; this file counts the method as the fourth part `source: derived`.

7. **Learn from the member VSLs — patterns across nine examples** (S-14 · lessons 181–190;
   `source: derived` from his choice of examples):
   - almost all name a method and give it 4–5 numbered steps;
   - most insert client testimonial clips between steps rather than at the end;
   - several state a **qualification floor** before the offer (for example, a minimum
     monthly revenue, or a minimum ad budget) and say who the offer is not for;
   - several close on "you could do this yourself, or have us do it";
   - one is a long free-value video (a full content strategy explained step by step, with a
     PDF) that ends with a low-pressure offer — the indirect lead magnet as a VSL;
   - nearly all use the "work for free until it works" guarantee (risk: H-15);
   - quality of production varies widely; he presents rough ones as working because the
     framing is right (lesson 240: messaging over polish).

8. **Keep attention moving** (S-14 · lesson 175 "How To Keep Engagement HIGH And Improve
   Ads/VSL"). Something should change about every 2–3 seconds in an ad and about every
   10 seconds in a VSL — a graphic, b-roll, a cut-in, a new angle, a gesture
   `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`. His reasoning: lower engagement raises cost per click,
   then cost per lead, then cost per sale, because you pay more for the same viewers.

9. **Produce for the feed** (S-14 · lesson 176 "5 Things To Test In Your Ads"; 310; 343):
   audience named in the on-screen headline; captions burned in (he says 60–70% watch
   without sound) `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14`; high-intent messaging (say what you
   offer and to whom, so curious non-buyers do not click and train the platform on the wrong
   people); 1080×1350 portrait or 1080×1080 square rather than full vertical, to fit the
   most placements `[tag: held-open]` against current placement specs; test a fast cutting
   style against a slow one, and professional footage against phone footage — which wins
   depends on the market. Pick a clickable thumbnail, because feed video does not always
   autoplay (343).

10. **If you will not present it yourself, hire a presenter** (S-14 · lesson 177 "Hiring A
    Spokesperson For Your Ad/VSL"): a freelance spokesperson from a marketplace, filtered to
    top-rated, chosen to resemble your buyer, priced by the script's word count; then add
    captions and edit.

11. **On camera, five habits** (S-14 · lesson 178 "5 Ways To Sound And Look More Natural On
    Camera"): record at least five full takes and pick the moment that lands; use your hands
    but vary the gestures; look away now and then as in a real conversation (holding an
    object helps); **read – think – say** each sentence in chunks; review every take once
    with sound only (listen for filler words) and once with picture only (watch how you
    look).

12. **Research what is running** (S-14 · lesson 174 "Do Market Research Using The Ad
    Library"; lesson 329; 345): search Meta's ad library for your niche plus "marketing";
    treat a long-running ad as a sign it works, since nobody pays to keep a loser on; open
    the funnel behind it; **model, do not copy** — and borrow from other industries and
    translate into yours, so it is not a copy.

13. **Draft with AI, finish by hand** (S-14 · lessons 180 "Using Our Custom AI VSL
    Copywriter", 193 "Using Our Custom AI Ad Copywriter", 232). His custom assistants ask for
    name, niche and offer statement, pillars, differentiators, offer and guarantee, proof,
    credibility, tone and top pains, and return a VSL draft or both ad scripts; he says
    they get you about 70–80% of the way `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14` and the rest is
    review and rewriting. Ad copy is the script turned into text.

14. **Diversify the creative** (S-14 · lesson 173; lesson 199 "Ad Example #6 (Photos)").
    Turn each script into a video, an image and a carousel; in images, a direct question to
    the business owner with a red arrow or circle stops the scroll. Since the 2025 change,
    many genuinely different creatives beat many hook-swaps of one video `[tag: conflict]`
    with lesson 168's "swap the hook first" (H-ED16-1).

15. **Expect the ad to matter more than the VSL** (S-14 · lesson 227): his own VSL failed on
    the first test and worked on the second with the same VSL and new ads — how the ad
    pre-frames the viewer decided it `[tag: assumption] stated by S-14, one test`.

## Decision points
- Strong results and a guarantee → bottom-of-funnel / offer-forward and a VSL. Few results →
  indirect free system or a case study, and more nurture.
- Hook not stopping the scroll (high cost per click, low click-through) → new hooks, then new
  formats; body or call to action only after.
- Buyer has heard your category a hundred times → name the method; never lead with the
  generic category name.

## Outputs
A lead magnet, a set of hooks per body, top/middle/bottom ad scripts, a seven-part VSL, a
set of formats per script, and recorded takes reviewed by sound and by picture.

## Open edges
- The 3/17/20/60 split has no stated source (H-ED17-1).
- Placement sizes and the 2025 "creative diversity" rule are Meta-specific and date fast
  (H-ED16-2).
- **Risky plays in the examples — do not copy into our course:** the "work for free until
  it works" guarantee (H-15); unsourced statistics presented as fact (one member VSL quotes
  survey percentages on humour in advertising with no source; lesson 328 opens on an
  unsourced "90%"); "only three spots" scarcity that may not be real; outcome claims in
  ads ("a flood of new customers", follower and revenue results) that need substantiation
  and, for health, finance, housing or employment niches, fall under Meta's stricter
  policies; and a presenter chosen to "look like" the audience must not imply they are a
  real customer.
- The member examples show results we cannot verify (follower growth, revenue, deals
  closed) — treat as `[tag: assumption]`.

## Canon map
**ACE Acquisition:** the hook's call-out is the first filter and the lead magnet choice sets
which part of the market is captured; the form questions and the source creative are the
"tag at capture" (ED-16 step 8) `source: derived`. **ACE Creation:** the seven-part VSL is a
recorded first half of a sales conversation — opening and call-out (rapport and fit), erase
doubts (objection pre-handling), name the method and reveal it (present the solution), offer
and guarantee (the ask) — so the live call (ED-07) starts after the prospect has already seen
the pillars; map it against the canon's seven-step script and keep the live script as the
authority `source: derived`. **ACE Expansion:** served clients supply the epiphany bridges,
testimonials and case studies every stage of this file depends on; no client result, no
bottom-of-funnel ad. See `registers/canon-map.md`.
